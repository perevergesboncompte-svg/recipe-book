# Masa Fina, Pepe Solla

- Section: empanadas
- Status: drafted
- Yield: 1 large empanada, base + tapa
- Active time: 15 min
- Rest: 30 min chilled
- Oven: 180 °C, 45 to 50 min
- Energy cost: $2.75 (50 min bake plus 30 min, 3.5 kW at 59 cents/kWh)
- Source: Pepe Solla, Casa Solla, Poio

## Ingredients

- Wheat flour: 650 g
- Butter, or manteca, melted: 150 g
- Water: 150 ml
- Olive oil: 50 ml
- White wine: 25 ml
- Eggs, in the dough: 2
- Egg, for brushing: 1
- Salt: a pinch

## Method

1. Melt the butter or manteca and let it cool to warm.
2. Mix the flour with the salt, then add the eggs, melted butter, oil, water and wine.
3. Knead to a smooth, even dough. It is rich and slightly greasy rather than sticky.
4. Wrap it and rest about 30 min in the fridge so it rolls more easily.
5. Divide in two and roll thin, base and tapa. Fill with cold relleno and seal.
6. Brush with beaten egg and bake at 180 °C for 45 to 50 min, until golden.

## Notes

- The restaurant reference for a thin, crisp empanada: no yeast, a lot of fat and a lot of egg, which gives a short, rich, brittle texture rather than a bready one.
- Water hydration is low at 27%, that is 150 ml water plus 25 ml wine against 650 g flour. It is offset by 200 g of fat, the melted butter plus the olive oil, at 31% of flour weight — the highest fat ratio of the six. That trade is what makes it crisp instead of leathery.
- Butter is finer and tastes of dairy. Manteca de cerdo is flakier and more traditional. Either works.
- No yeast, so nothing ferments. The cold rest is only to make it roll better.
- White wine adds flavour and helps the crispness.
- Roll it genuinely thin. With that much fat it cooks fast and comes out flaky.
- Pepe Solla, Casa Solla, Poio, 1 Michelin star, the oldest in Galicia.
