# Fruit Galettes

- Section: baking
- Status: drafted
- Image: images/peach-galette.jpg
- Makes: 2 large galettes, 35 cm, or 12 small ones at 15 to 18 cm
- Active time: 1 h
- Rest: 1 h chilled for the dough, then two 10 min chills once rolled and filled
- Total time: about 3 h 30 min
- Oven: 375F / 190C, 45 to 60 min for large galettes or 40 to 50 min for small
- Source: Tartine, A Classic Revisited

## Ingredients

- Unsalted butter, very cold: 450 g
- Water: 240 ml
- Salt: 1 1/2 tsp
- Pastry flour: 340 g
- All-purpose flour: 340 g
- Fruit, prepared to suit the type: 910 g
- Granulated or brown sugar, for the fruit: 25 to 50 g per large galette, or 1 to 2 tsp per small
- Lemon juice, for the fruit that needs it: quantity not given
- Egg yolks, large, for the wash: 2
- Heavy cream, for the wash: 2 tbsp
- Granulated sugar, for sprinkling: quantity not given

## Method

1. Cut the butter into 2.5 cm cubes and send them to the freezer. Stir the salt into the water until it dissolves and freeze that too. Both want about 10 minutes.
2. Weigh both flours straight onto the bench. There is no need to combine them first, because the making will do that for you. Spread them out to a rectangle roughly 8 mm deep.
3. Scatter the cold butter cubes across the flour, then throw a little flour back over the butter so the pin has nothing to grab, and start rolling.
4. Once the cubes have gone long and thin, lift the edges of the rectangle back toward the middle with a bench scraper so you are working the same footprint you began with. Roll and gather like this three or four times.
5. Open a well in the centre and tip in all of the water at once. Scrape the sides inward, cutting the water through the flour and butter with the scraper, until you have a shaggy mass.
6. Pat it into a rectangle of about 25 by 35 cm and dust the top with flour.
7. Roll the rectangle out to half again its size, gather the top, bottom and sides back to the starting footprint with the scraper, and roll it out again. Three or four rounds of this gives a smooth, cohesive dough, finishing as a neat 25 by 35 cm rectangle.
8. Slide the dough onto a large baking sheet, cover it with plastic wrap and chill it well, about 1 hour.
9. Prepare the fruit while the dough is in the fridge. Hull berries if they need it. Pit peaches and cut them into eighths. Pit apricots and leave them halved or cut them into quarters. Pit cherries. Sauté apples or pears and let them cool completely.
10. Line two baking sheets with parchment paper.
11. Cut the chilled dough into two equal pieces for large galettes, or twelve for individual ones.
12. To get a circle out of a rough square, set a piece in front of you as a diamond, with the handles of the pin over two opposite points. Roll from the middle out toward each of those two points, flattening only the centre and leaving the near and far points thick.
13. Turn the dough a quarter turn, so the two flattened corners now sit nearest and farthest from you, and roll from the middle again, stopping short of both ends.
14. You will be left with a square carrying small humps between the pointed corners. Roll those humps down and the circle appears. Large circles finish a shade over 3 mm thick and about 35 cm across, small ones a little thinner at 15 to 18 cm.
15. Fold each large circle into quarters to carry it, lay it on a lined sheet and open it out again. Small circles can be moved as they are. Chill the circles until firm, about 10 minutes.
16. Heap fruit into the middle of each circle, leaving a bare rim of 5 cm on a large one and 2.5 cm on a small one. Mound berries rather than laying them flat, because they shed a lot of volume in the oven.
17. Taste the fruit first to judge how sweet it needs to be, then sugar each galette on its own sheet with granulated or brown sugar, generally 25 to 50 g for a large galette and 1 to 2 tsp for a small one. Blueberries, blackberries and sautéed apples or pears also take a squeeze of lemon.
18. Bring the bare rim up over the fruit so it is partly covered. Press out any dip or channel in the folds, since that is where juice will find its way out. Chill again until firm, 10 minutes.
19. Set oven racks at the middle and the lower third and heat the oven to 375F / 190C.
20. Whisk the yolks and the cream together in a small bowl. Brush it over the folded pastry and scatter granulated sugar on top.
21. Bake until the crust has clearly risen and gone dark brown and you can see the fruit juices bubbling, 45 to 60 minutes for a large galette and 40 to 50 for a small one. Halfway through, turn each sheet end for end and trade the sheets between the two racks so they colour evenly.
22. Should the pastry be browning ahead of the fruit, take the oven down to 350F / 180C or cover the tops with aluminium foil.
23. Serve them straight from the oven, or cool them on a wire rack and eat them warm or at room temperature.

## Notes

- Half pastry flour and half all-purpose by weight makes the best crust. With no pastry flour on hand, 680 g of all-purpose alone still works.
- Sweeten each galette individually on the sheet. Tossing the whole 910 g of fruit with sugar in a bowl draws the juice out before it ever reaches the pastry, and you want that juice inside the galette.
- Apricots carry less water than most fruit, so they concentrate hard in the oven and their cut tips blacken. Yellow peaches and nectarines bake well. White varieties are subtle and low in acid, which is better raw.
- Berries work alone or alongside stone fruit. In winter the filling is sautéed apples or pears, or poached quince.
- Frangipane cream spread thinly under the fruit is the classic pairing and makes a more substantial pastry.
- Assembled galettes can go into the oven at once or sit unwrapped in the fridge for a couple of hours. To freeze them, leave off the wash and the sugar, wrap them airtight and keep up to 2 weeks. Wash, sugar and bake straight from frozen, adding 10 minutes.
- Baked galettes keep 2 days at room temperature or 5 days refrigerated. Five minutes at 400F / 200C crisps them up again.
