# Chocolate Hazelnut Tart

- Section: baking
- Status: drafted
- Dough: sweet-tart-dough
- Makes: 1 tart at 23 cm or 6 to 8 tartlets at 10 cm, 8 to 12 servings
- Active time: 25 min
- Total time: about 1 h 30 min including cooling, not counting the shell
- Oven: 325F/160C for 7 to 9 min
- Source: Tartine, A Classic Revisited

## Ingredients

- Sweet tart dough, baked and cooled: 1 batch
- Bittersweet chocolate, coarsely chopped: 170 g
- Unsalted butter: 115 g
- Brandy, optional: 60 ml
- Sugar, for the butter mixture: 70 g
- Sugar, for the eggs: 65 g
- Eggs, large: 3
- Orange zest: 1/2 tsp
- Salt: 1/8 tsp
- Hazelnuts, lightly toasted and rubbed free of their skins: 140 g

## Method

1. Make the sweet tart dough, line a 23 cm tart pan or six to eight 10 cm tartlet pans, bake the shells through and cool them. See the dough.
2. Heat the oven to 325F/160C and have the cooled shell ready.
3. Tip the chopped chocolate into a heatproof mixing bowl.
4. Put the butter, the brandy if you are using it, and 70 g of the sugar in a saucepan. Stir over medium-low heat until the butter has melted and the sugar is fully dissolved.
5. Pour the hot butter mixture onto the chocolate and stir it with a rubber spatula to a smooth melt.
6. Put the remaining 65 g of sugar in a large bowl with the eggs, the orange zest and the salt.
7. Beat on medium speed with the whisk attachment, or by hand with a whisk, about 3 minutes. The mixture should turn very pale and light and drop from the lifted whisk in a thick ribbon.
8. Whisk a third of the beaten eggs into the chocolate to loosen it, then fold the remainder in with a rubber spatula.
9. Pour the filling into the shell and level the top with an offset spatula.
10. Lay the hazelnuts over the surface in an even layer.
11. Bake 7 to 9 minutes, until the filling has lost some of its shine.
12. Let the tart cool all the way down on a rack, then serve it at room temperature.

## Notes

- Sugar is 135 g in total, split two ways. 70 g dissolves into the warm butter and brandy, the other 65 g is beaten with the eggs.
- Beating the eggs to a ribbon is what keeps the filling light under 170 g of chocolate.
- The filling can be made ahead and refrigerated. Spoon it into the shell cold, smooth the top and bake as written.
- Variations on the same filling: equal weights of coffee and walnuts in place of the brandy and hazelnuts, or prunes soaked in Armagnac laid into the shell before the filling goes in, with the brandy and nuts left out.
- Keeps well wrapped in the refrigerator up to 5 days.
