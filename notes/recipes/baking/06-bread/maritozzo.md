# Maritozzo

- Section: baking
- Status: drafted
- Image: images/maritozzo.jpg
- Yield: 12 maritozzi
- Unit weight: 60-65 g of dough each
- Active time: 30 min
- Rest: 1 h at room temperature, then several hours or overnight chilled, then 2.5-3 h final rise
- Total time: about 7 h
- Oven: 375F, 15-20 min
- Energy cost: $1.72 (20 min bake plus 30 min, 3.5 kW at 59 cents/kWh)
- Source: King Arthur Baking, kingarthurbaking.com/recipes/maritozzi-recipe

## Ingredients

- All-purpose flour: 330 g
- Dry milk powder: 28 g
- Granulated sugar: 35 g
- Table salt: 8 g
- Instant yeast: 1 tbsp
- Eggs, cold from the fridge: 3 large
- Water, cool: 57 g
- Unsalted butter, cool room temperature, in 5 pieces: 142 g
- Egg, beaten with 1 tbsp water, to glaze: 1 large
- Heavy cream: 227 g
- Confectioners' sugar: 35 g, plus more to finish

## Method

1. Mix and knead everything except the butter to a smooth, stretchy dough that clears the bowl, 7 to 10 minutes on medium.
2. Add the butter one piece at a time, letting each disappear before the next. Expect another 5 to 10 minutes beyond a normal dough.
3. Ball it up, cover, rise 1 hour at room temperature, then refrigerate several hours or overnight. The chill firms the butter and makes it handleable.
4. Divide the cold dough into 12 pieces of 60 to 65 g and roll each into a tight ball under a cupped hand.
5. Space across two lined trays, cover, rise 2.5 to 3 hours until doubled and puffy.
6. Heat the oven to 375F. Brush with egg wash just before baking.
7. Bake 15 to 20 minutes until golden, tenting at 10 minutes if they colour fast. Stand 5 minutes, then cool completely on a rack.
8. Whip the cream to soft peaks, add the confectioners' sugar, take it to stiff peaks without overbeating.
9. Slit each cooled bun lengthwise, keeping the base intact.
10. Pipe or spoon in 2 to 3 tbsp of cream, then level it flush with the cut edge using an offset spatula.
11. Dust heavily with confectioners' sugar.

## Notes

- Dry milk powder plus 142 g butter to 330 g flour is what makes this an enriched brioche rather than a bun.
- Three cold eggs and only 57 g water, so the eggs are most of the hydration.
- The overnight chill is structural, not for flavour. This dough is too soft to shape warm.
- Levelling the cream flush is the traditional finish, not a flourish.
- Unfilled buns freeze and double as burger buns or sandwich rolls.
- Filled ones are a same-day proposition.
