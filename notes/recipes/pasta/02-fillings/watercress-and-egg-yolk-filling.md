# Watercress and Egg Yolk Filling

- Section: pasta
- Status: drafted
- Chef: Ruth Hansom
- Source: Great British Chefs

## Ingredients

- Watercress mixture — watercress (leaves picked, stalks reserved): 6 handfuls
- Watercress mixture — onion: 1
- Watercress mixture — lime, zested: 1
- Watercress mixture — pecorino (or Lord of The Hundreds): 50 g
- Watercress mixture — mascarpone: 2 tbsp
- Watercress mixture — salt and pepper: to taste
- Egg yolk — egg yolks, large and raw: 3

## Method

1. Pick the watercress leaves and set the stalks aside. Hold back a few small leaves for garnish.
2. Blanch the rest 30 seconds, chill in iced water, then wring out every drop in a tea towel or muslin. Chop the blanched leaves.
3. Finely chop half the onion and roughly chop the other half.
4. Stir the finely chopped onion into the watercress with the mascarpone and lime zest. Grate in the pecorino and season.
5. Shape into 2 pucks 1.5 cm high with an 8 cm round cutter, and chill.
6. At assembly, press a small well into each puck and nestle a raw yolk in it.
7. Beat the third yolk and brush it around the filling as the seal.

## Notes

- Used in Watercress and Egg Yolk Ravioli, as one large raviolo per person.
- Two sub-components: a shaped watercress puck and a raw yolk that sits inside it. The yolk stays raw and turns into the sauce when the raviolo is cut, so the trick is getting it in without breaking it.
- The 3 yolks split 2 inside and 1 beaten for the wash.
- Onion accounting is muddled in the source. Half is finely chopped here, and the roughly chopped half then appears twice — once in the sauce base and again as "remaining onion" fried with the wild mushrooms at plating.
- The reserved stalks go to the mushroom sauce, outside this file.
- Squeezing the watercress properly dry is what keeps the puck holding its shape.
