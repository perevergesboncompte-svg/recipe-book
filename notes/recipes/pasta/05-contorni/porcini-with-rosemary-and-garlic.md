# Porcini with Rosemary and Garlic

- Section: pasta
- Status: drafted
- Serves: 4-6
- Total time: 45 min
- Source: Missy Robbins, Pasta

## Ingredients

- Olive oil, for the caramelized garlic: 56 g
- Garlic, 15 whole cloves: 75 g
- Porcini mushrooms, about 8: 1.2 kg
- Olive oil, for the chopped garlic: 42 g
- Olive oil, for searing the porcini: 56 g
- Olive oil, for drizzling: quantity not given
- Garlic, finely chopped: 30 g
- Salt: quantity not given
- Lemon, halved and seeds removed: 1
- Lemon peel, pith removed and finely chopped: 1/2 lemon
- Rosemary, leaves picked: 3 sprigs
- Flaky sea salt: quantity not given

## Method

1. For the caramelized garlic, pour the olive oil into a large sauté pan set over low heat, then lay the whole cloves out in a single layer. Cook them slowly 7 to 10 minutes, stirring so they cook evenly, until light golden and soft enough to mash.
2. Take the pan off the heat and let the garlic cool in its oil. It will keep in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days.
3. Clean the porcini by paring the outer layer of flesh off the stems with a paring knife, taking as little as you can. Wipe away whatever dirt is left with a slightly damp towel.
4. Halve the mushrooms lengthwise.
5. Warm the 42 g of olive oil in a sauté pan over low heat and cook the chopped garlic 10 to 15 seconds, to aromatic and still pale. Set it aside.
6. Set a large sauté pan over medium heat with the remaining 56 g of olive oil. Lay the porcini in cut side down in a single layer and press a second pan on top to weight them, which keeps them from curling as they hit the heat. After 1 to 2 minutes lift the top pan off and carry on 2 to 3 minutes, to golden brown and glossy.
7. Turn the mushrooms and give them another 2 to 3 minutes, until browned and cooked through but not mushy. Add a little more oil if the pan dries out. In the last minute season both sides with salt and toss them.
8. Lift the caramelized cloves out of their oil into a large bowl and mash them lightly under a fork.
9. Tip in the mushrooms and the gently cooked chopped garlic, squeeze the juice from both lemon halves over and stir it together.
10. Arrange the porcini cut side up on a serving platter or divide them onto plates. Finish with the chopped lemon peel, the rosemary leaves, a drizzle of olive oil and flaky sea salt.

## Notes

- Porcini can be difficult to get hold of, and nothing else quite matches their flavor or texture. King oyster, sold as trumpet royale, is the closest substitute.
- Clean the stems with a paring knife rather than water, and shave off only the first layer.
- The second pan on top is there because porcini curl when they hit the heat, which costs you an even sear.
- Mushrooms soak up a lot of oil, so expect to add more partway through the sear.
