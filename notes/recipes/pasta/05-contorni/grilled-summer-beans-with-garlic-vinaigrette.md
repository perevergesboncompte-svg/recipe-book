# Grilled Summer Beans and Garlic Vinaigrette

- Section: pasta
- Status: drafted
- Serves: 6-8
- Total time: 40 min
- Source: Missy Robbins, Pasta

## Ingredients

- Shallot, finely diced: 25 g
- Olive oil, for the vinaigrette: 150 g
- Garlic, finely chopped: 125 g
- Marjoram, finely chopped: 6 g
- Parsley, finely chopped: 15 g
- Dried red chile flakes, for the vinaigrette: 1/4 tsp
- Red wine vinegar: 55 g
- Summer beans, trimmed: 794 g
- Olive oil, for the beans: 42 g
- Salt: 1/2 tsp
- Dried red chile flakes, to finish: 1/4 tsp
- Flaky sea salt: quantity not given

## Method

1. For the vinaigrette, put the 150 g of olive oil and the diced shallot into a small saucepan over the lowest heat and cook 5 to 8 minutes, until the shallot has softened at the edges and turned completely translucent. Add the garlic and carry on 2 to 3 minutes more, stirring often, until the shallot is soft all the way through and the garlic has softened without taking color.
2. Tip the contents of the pan into a heatproof bowl and let it cool.
3. Once it is cool, stir in the marjoram, parsley and chile flakes. That is the vinaigrette base. Measure out 80 g for this dish and keep the rest in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 5 days.
4. Get the grill up to high, or put a grill pan on the stove top over high heat.
5. Toss the beans in a large bowl with the 42 g of olive oil and the salt. Keep it light, since flaky salt finishes the dish.
6. Spread the beans in an even layer on a grill topper or in a grill basket so none fall through the grate. Cook them 2 to 4 minutes a side, turning once, until they char and soften but keep some crunch. In a grill pan, work in batches rather than crowding it.
7. While they cook, pour the 80 g of reserved base into a small bowl or measuring cup and stir the red wine vinegar through it.
8. Move the cooked beans into a large bowl, then add the vinaigrette and the chile flakes and stir to coat.
9. Transfer to a serving platter and finish with flaky sea salt.

## Notes

- Runner beans, haricots verts and wax beans all work here, and so does most any vegetable coming off the grill.
- A grill is not required. A grill pan or a sauté pan over high heat gets there, as long as the beans take on a real char and blister on the outside.
- The vinegar goes in only at the last minute, so the leftover base keeps as a plain garlic oil. Add vinegar to it later for a salad dressing, a marinade or a warm vinaigrette for fish.
- Season the raw beans lightly. Most of the seasoning comes from the flaky salt at the end.
