# Tagliolini

- Section: pasta
- Status: drafted
- Formed: ribbon
- Dough: egg-dough
- Region: Widespread across Italy under various local names, as far south as Molise and as far north as Trentino-Alto Adige
- Rest: 20-30 min at room temperature
- Source: Pasta, Missy Robbins

## Method

1. Make an egg-based dough, then roll and sheet it.
2. Give a wooden work surface a light dusting of 00 flour. Line a sheet pan with parchment, then dust the parchment with semolina.
3. Knife the sheets into 18-inch lengths and trim the ragged edges off. Save the trim for soup. Keep the sheets under plastic or a towel.
4. Fit the tagliolini cutter to a hand-crank machine or the KitchenAid attachment.
5. Hold one end of a sheet, feed the other end into the cutter, and crank, keeping the sheet nearly perpendicular to the rollers. Catch the strands underneath with your free hand.
6. Dust the strands generously with 00 flour and shake them apart. Coil the batch into a nest on the pan.
7. Repeat with the rest of the sheets.
8. Dry 20 to 30 minutes at room temperature.
9. To hold, cover the pan with plastic and refrigerate up to 24 hours.

## Notes

- To cut by hand instead of by machine: fold a sheet in half, then in thirds, into a packet about 4 1/2 by 6 inches. Turn it so the long side faces you, trim the ends, then cut 1/8-inch strands working inward from one edge. Rejoin the method at the dusting step.
- Hand-cut width is 1/8 inch. Robbins normally reaches for the machine's tagliolini cutter for this shape rather than cutting by hand.
- Rolled a little thicker than tajarin or maccheroncini di Campofilone, the two hand-cut members of the same family. Of the three, this is the easiest starting point.
- Known as tajarin in Piedmont and maccheroncini di Campofilone in the Marche.
- Coiled into a nest, not a horseshoe.
- Refrigerated shelf life is 24 hours.
- Goes with light seafood sauces, crab above all. It also holds up in a minimal dressing of lemon, butter, and parmigiano (al limone).
