# Canederli

- Section: pasta
- Status: drafted
- Formed: dumpling
- Region: Italian Alps, particularly Trentino–Alto Adige
- Source: Pasta, Missy Robbins

## Method

1. Line a sheet pan with parchment.
2. Wet your hands lightly and roll the canederli dough into 54 g balls, about golf-ball size. Work in batches. Set them on the pan as you go.
3. Cover with plastic wrap and refrigerate up to 24 hours.

## Notes

- No tool beyond your hands. The defining element is the dough: stale bread rather than a rolled or extruded flour dough. Locally these are called gnocchi di pane, bread gnocchi.
- Portion weight is 54 g per ball.
- The book's version uses rye bread in place of white.
- Known as Knödel across the Austrian and German border. Two traditional variants: canederli di speck, with smoked cured ham, bread, eggs, flour, and chives; and canederli di spinaci, with cheese, spinach, and parsley.
- Can be boiled ahead, cooled on a sheet pan, then covered and refrigerated up to 24 hours before serving. Raw formed balls also keep refrigerated up to 24 hours.
- Served in sage-and-butter sauce or in brodo.
