# Canederli Dough

- Section: pasta
- Status: drafted
- Makes: 18 pieces
- Active time: 30 min
- Rest: 30 min to 1 h chilled, after an overnight dry for the bread
- Source: Pasta, Missy Robbins

## Ingredients

- Jewish-style rye bread: 397 g
- Milk: 474 g
- Olive oil: 56 g
- Onions, finely diced: 204 g
- Eggs, mixed: 200 g
- Speck, in a slab, finely diced: 85 g
- All-purpose flour: 40 g
- Parsley, finely chopped: 10 g

## Method

1. Cut the bread into 1-inch cubes and spread them on a sheet pan to dry out overnight. If you need them sooner, hold the pan in a 200°F oven for an hour, long enough to harden without taking colour. Cool.
2. Tip the cubes into a large bowl, pour the milk over and leave 20 to 30 minutes, until no hard centres remain.
3. Meanwhile set a sauté pan over medium-low heat with the oil and onions. Give them 15 to 20 minutes, to the point where they are only starting to caramelise. Turn them out into a bowl and let them cool.
4. Fold the cooled onions into the soaked bread along with the eggs, speck, flour and parsley. Press out any lump of bread that has survived, so the mixture is even throughout.
5. Cover and chill 30 minutes to 1 hour before rolling into balls.

## Notes

- This is a bread dumpling mixture rather than a flour-and-egg dough, so there is no kneading and no gluten to develop.
- The overnight dry matters more than it looks. Bread still soft in the middle turns to paste once the milk goes in.
- Let the onions cool before they meet the eggs, or the eggs start to set.
- Spinach is the usual swap for the speck.
- Rolls into 18 canederli at roughly 54 g each.

## Preserving

Form the surplus into balls before freezing rather than keeping the loose mixture.
A wet bread mix does not hold well once chilled past a day.

- Roll the whole batch, then freeze the balls apart on a tray before bagging them, so they do not fuse.
- Cook from frozen, allowing a few extra minutes at a bare simmer.
- The unformed mixture is best used the day it is made.
