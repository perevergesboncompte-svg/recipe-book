# Potato and Crème Fraîche-Filled Ravioli with Garlic and Rosemary

- Section: pasta
- Status: drafted
- Shape: ravioli
- Filling: potato-and-creme-fraiche-filling
- Serves: 6-8
- Total time: 2 h 30 plus the dough rest
- Source: Missy Robbins, Pasta

## Ingredients

- Egg dough: 1 batch
- Yukon gold potatoes: 228 g
- Ricotta: 154 g
- Crème fraîche: 225 g
- Parmigiano-reggiano, finely grated, for the filling: 22 g
- Lemon peel, pith removed and finely chopped: 1/4 lemon
- Olive oil, for frying the garlic: 224 g
- Garlic, sliced 1/16 inch thick on a mandoline, for the crispy garlic: 50 g
- Olive oil, for the sauce: 28 g
- Garlic, thinly sliced: 10 g
- Unsalted butter, cold and cubed: 60 g
- Parmigiano-reggiano, finely grated, to finish: 30 g
- Rosemary, leaves picked: 2 sprigs
- Salt: quantity not given

## Method

1. Put the potatoes in a pot, cover them with cold water and bring them to a simmer over medium heat. Give them 30 to 45 minutes, until a fork goes through without resistance.
2. Lift them out with a slotted spoon onto a sheet pan and leave them 15 minutes. They dry out a little as they cool.
3. Peel them while they are still warm enough to handle, then pass them through a ricer or food mill into a bowl.
4. Push the riced potato through a tamis or a fine-mesh strainer a second time so it is perfectly smooth.
5. Pulse the ricotta in a food processor to the texture of cake frosting, stopping the moment it turns glossy. Going further breaks it. Scrape it into a bowl.
6. When the potato is completely cold, fold in the ricotta, the crème fraîche, the parmigiano and the lemon peel, just enough to combine. Any more working and the potato turns gummy. Salt to taste and refrigerate until you are ready to pipe.
7. Line a plate with paper towels. For the crispy garlic, pour the 224 g of oil into a saute pan over low heat, slide the garlic in and cook 3 to 5 minutes, stirring now and then, until the slices take color and firm up. They curl and cluster as they go, so keep moving them or they color unevenly.
8. Scoop the garlic out with a slotted spoon and spread it in one layer on the paper to drain. Slices that are still soft will crisp as they dry.
9. Make 80 to 100 ravioli with the egg dough and the filling.
10. Salt a large pot of water heavily and bring it to the boil over high heat.
11. Slide the ravioli in and pull the heat back so the water only simmers. A rolling boil splits this filling. Cook 2 to 3 minutes. Test the thickest closure, which should give tenderly.
12. While they cook, set a large saute pan over low heat with the 28 g of oil. Add the sliced garlic and give it 10 to 15 seconds, until it smells but takes no color.
13. Add the butter and three ladles of pasta water, 170 g, then swirl the pan to emulsify.
14. Lift the ravioli across with a spider, raise the heat to medium and swirl 30 to 45 seconds, turning them with a spoon so both faces are coated. A splash more pasta water loosens the sauce if it tightens. It is ready when it clings with a gloss.
15. Move them to a platter or divide onto plates, spoon the sauce left in the pan over the top and finish with the parmigiano, the rosemary and the crispy garlic.

## Notes

- These simmer, they never boil. The potato filling is loose and the seams give way over high heat.
- Pass the potato twice, through the ricer and then the tamis, and fold the dairy in only until it comes together. Overworked potato goes gummy.
- Whip the ricotta only to a frosting-like sheen. Past that point it breaks.
- The garlic chips keep crisping while they drain, so pull them while they are just gold and slightly hardened rather than fully brittle.
- The 224 g of oil the garlic fries in is not the sauce oil. The sauce takes its own 28 g.
