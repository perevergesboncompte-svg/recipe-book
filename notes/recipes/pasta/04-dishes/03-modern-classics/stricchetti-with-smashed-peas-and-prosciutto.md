# Stricchetti with Smashed Peas and Prosciutto

- Section: pasta
- Status: drafted
- Shape: stricchetti
- Serves: 4-6
- Total time: 1 h 30 min plus the dough rest
- Source: Missy Robbins, Pasta

## Ingredients

- Shelled English peas: 300 g
- Olive oil, for the peas: 56 g
- Egg dough: 1 batch
- Olive oil: 42 g
- Onion, finely diced: 370 g
- Garlic, finely chopped: 10 g
- Unsalted butter, cold and cubed: 75 g
- Parmigiano-reggiano, finely grated: 45 g
- Prosciutto, 40 g finely diced and 40 g julienned: 80 g
- Black pepper: quantity not given

## Method

1. Bring a small saucepan of water to the boil and salt it heavily.
2. Set up a salted ice-water bath with a colander standing in it. Salt in the bath is what holds the green of the peas.
3. Blanch the peas 1 to 2 minutes, until they start to soften, then lift them into the ice water with a spider. Leave the pan boiling, you need it twice more.
4. Once the peas have cooled, put them back through the boiling water and the ice bath two more times, until a fork or your fingertips crush them easily.
5. Take them out of the ice bath for the last time and turn them onto a kitchen towel to drain.
6. Tip the peas into a bowl and start mashing with a fork. Keep mashing while you drizzle in the 56 g olive oil, to a coarse mash with the texture of a rustic pesto. Set it aside.
7. Shape 624 g of stricchetti from the egg dough.
8. Salt a large pot of water heavily and bring it to the boil.
9. While the water heats, set a saute pan over low heat with the 42 g olive oil. Cook the onion gently 5 to 8 minutes, soft but without color, then add the garlic for 10 to 15 seconds, until it goes aromatic.
10. Stir the crushed peas into the onion and set the pan aside.
11. Cook the stricchetti 2 to 3 minutes. The center of the shape should be tender.
12. While they cook, put the pan back over low heat, add 3 ladles of pasta water, 170 g, and the butter, and swirl to emulsify.
13. Lift the pasta across with a spider, raise the heat to medium and toss 1 to 2 minutes to marry.
14. Off the heat, toss in half the parmigiano, then the diced prosciutto. A splash of pasta water loosens the sauce if it tightens. It is ready when it clings with a gloss.
15. Divide onto plates and finish with the julienned prosciutto, the rest of the parmigiano and pepper.

## Notes

- Blanching and shocking three times gets the peas soft enough to mash without costing them their color or flavor. One long boil would take both.
- The pea mash breaks down into the sauce once the pasta is married, so stop at rustic-pesto texture and do not take it smooth.
- The 80 g piece of prosciutto splits in two: 40 g diced into the pan, 40 g julienned over the top.
- Salt the ice bath as well as the boiling water.
- Stricchetti are the same shape as farfalle. Peas and prosciutto is a spring pairing in Emilia-Romagna, usually with this shape or tagliatelle.
