# Ravioli with Taleggio Fonduta, Peas, and Speck

- Section: pasta
- Status: drafted
- Shape: ravioli
- Filling: taleggio-fonduta-filling
- Serves: 4-6
- Total time: 3 h plus the dough rest
- Source: Missy Robbins, Pasta

## Ingredients

- Egg dough: 1 batch
- Heavy cream: 474 g
- Taleggio with rind: 340 g
- Egg yolk: 10 g
- English peas, shelled: 150 g
- Olive oil: 42 g
- Garlic, thinly sliced: 5 g
- Unsalted butter, cold and cubed: 60 g
- Speck, finely diced: 85 g
- Pea tendrils, tender: 20 g
- Parmigiano-reggiano, finely grated: 22 g
- Salt: quantity not given
- Black pepper: quantity not given

## Method

1. For the filling, bring the cream to a bare simmer in a saucepan over medium heat. Drop the heat to low and hold it there until the cream has reduced by a third, 1 to 1½ hours. Watch the bottom of the pan, it scorches easily. Let it stand 5 minutes.
2. While the cream reduces, cut the rind off the Taleggio and keep only the creamy interior. Chop that into 1-inch pieces.
3. Pour the cream into a high-speed blender. With it running on low to medium, feed the cheese in slowly until the mixture emulsifies. Taste before you salt, because Taleggio brings plenty of salinity on its own.
4. Still blending, add the egg yolk. Cover and refrigerate at least 1 hour. Filling that has sat several hours or overnight needs 30 minutes out of the fridge to soften before you pipe it.
5. Make 35 to 40 ravioli with the egg dough and the fonduta.
6. Salt a small pot of water heavily and bring it to the boil over high heat. Set up a salted ice bath with a colander standing in it.
7. Blanch the peas 20 to 30 seconds, until barely tender and still bright green. Move them into the ice bath with a slotted spoon to stop them cooking, then lift the colander out and set it aside.
8. Salt a large pot of water heavily and bring it to the boil over high heat.
9. Set a large saute pan over low heat with the oil. Add the garlic and give it 10 to 15 seconds, until it smells but takes no color. Stir the peas through and set the pan aside.
10. Slide the ravioli into the large pot and pull the heat back to a gentle simmer. A rolling boil breaks this filling. Cook 2 to 3 minutes. The thickest closure should give tenderly, and a ravioli cut open should run.
11. Meanwhile return the pea pan to medium-low heat. Drop the butter in with 115 to 170 g of pasta water, 2 to 3 ladles, and swirl to emulsify. Stir half the speck through.
12. Lift the ravioli across with a spider, raise the heat to medium and swirl 30 seconds to a minute, turning them with a spoon so both faces take the sauce. A splash more pasta water loosens it if it tightens.
13. Drop in half the pea tendrils and let them wilt in the pan, 10 to 15 seconds.
14. Divide onto plates and finish with the rest of the speck and tendrils, the parmigiano and pepper to taste.

## Notes

- This fonduta is cheese and cream with a little yolk, no wine, which is what gives it the loose texture that runs out of the pasta.
- Only the creamy interior of the Taleggio goes into the blender. Cut the rind away and discard it.
- Reducing the cream by a third takes 1 to 1½ hours on low on purpose. A scorched pan bottom carries straight through into the filling.
- The peas get 20 to 30 seconds and a salted ice bath. Longer and they lose both color and bite.
- Half the speck and half the tendrils cook into the sauce, the other half go on raw at the plate.
