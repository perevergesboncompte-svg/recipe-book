# Corzetti with Chanterelles and Aged Goat Cheese

- Section: pasta
- Status: drafted
- Shape: corzetti
- Serves: 4-6
- Total time: 1 h plus the dough rest
- Source: Missy Robbins, Pasta

## Ingredients

- Egg dough: 1 batch
- Olive oil: 75 g
- Chanterelles, cleaned: 454 g
- Garlic, finely chopped: 10 g
- Dry white wine: 220 g
- Unsalted butter, cold and cubed: 45 g
- Thyme, leaves picked: 5 sprigs
- Aged Italian goat cheese, coarsely grated: 57 g

## Method

1. Stamp 80 corzetti from the egg dough.
2. Leave small and medium chanterelles whole and halve or quarter the big ones.
3. Warm the oil in a large saute pan over medium heat, add the mushrooms and turn them so every piece is coated. Give them 2 to 3 minutes to start going soft.
4. Stir the garlic through and let it cook 20 to 30 seconds.
5. Pour in the wine and cook 5 to 8 minutes, until almost nothing is left in the pan and the mushrooms are tender without being mushy. Keep whatever juice they have given up, it becomes the sauce. Set the pan aside.
6. Salt a large pot of water heavily and bring it to the boil.
7. Cook the corzetti 1 to 2 minutes. They want to be tender, not soft.
8. While they boil, set the mushroom pan back over low heat and stir in two ladles of the pasta water, about 115 g.
9. Lift the pasta across with a spider, raise the heat to medium, add the butter and swirl for about a minute, turning the corzetti with a spoon so every face gets coated. A splash more pasta water loosens the sauce if it tightens. It is ready when it clings with a gloss.
10. Plate in a single layer and finish with the thyme and the grated cheese.

## Notes

- Two aged goat cheeses from Sardinia suit this: pantaleo is the more herbal, capra sarda the richer and deeper. Both carry a buttery, lemony note.
- Chanterelles run from early summer to late autumn, which is the window for this one.
- The pan juice the mushrooms release is half the sauce, so do not cook the wine so hard that the pan goes dry.
