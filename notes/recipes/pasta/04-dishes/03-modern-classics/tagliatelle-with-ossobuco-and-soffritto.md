# Tagliatelle with Ossobuco and Soffritto

- Section: pasta
- Status: drafted
- Shape: tagliatelle
- Serves: 4-6
- Total time: 5 h plus the salting and the dough rest
- Source: Missy Robbins, Pasta

## Ingredients

- Veal shanks, center cut, in 2-inch-thick pieces: 910 g
- Salt: 14 g
- Black pepper: quantity not given
- Fennel bulb: 400 g
- Carrot: 150 g
- Onion: 370 g
- Olive oil: 103 g
- Garlic, thinly sliced: 20 g
- Dry white wine: 360 g
- Whole San Marzano tomatoes, crushed by hand: 794 g
- Fennel seed: 6 g
- Dried red chile flakes: 4 g
- Rosemary: 5 sprigs
- Thyme: 2 sprigs
- Egg dough: 1 batch
- Fennel pollen: quantity not given
- Parmigiano-reggiano, finely grated: 22 g

## Method

1. Season the shanks with the 14 g salt and leave them 1 to 2 hours, or refrigerate overnight if you have the time. Pepper them just before they go into the pot.
2. Core the fennel and strip off the fronds and stems. Peel the carrot. Cut the fennel, carrot and onion into 1/2- to 1-inch pieces.
3. Run the fennel, onion and carrot through a meat grinder fitted with the small die. Gather the ground vegetables in cheesecloth and wring the liquid out of them. That is your soffritto.
4. Put the olive oil into a large heavy sauce pot or Dutch oven and set it over medium heat. Sear the shanks, turning as needed, until golden brown on every side, 7 to 9 minutes. Lift them onto a plate and scrape any burnt residue out of the pot.
5. Turn the heat to low, add the soffritto and cook gently, stirring often, 5 to 8 minutes, until it has softened but taken no color. Add the garlic right at the end and give it 10 to 15 seconds, just long enough to go aromatic.
6. Return the shanks and pour in the wine. Simmer 5 to 8 minutes, until almost all of it has gone.
7. Add the tomatoes with their juice, then enough water to sit an inch above the shanks. Tie the fennel seed, chile flakes, rosemary and thyme into a cheesecloth sachet and drop it in.
8. Bring it to a simmer over low heat, cover the pot and cook 2 to 3 hours, either on the stove top or in a 275°F oven. The meat is done when it slips off the bone but has not gone to mush. Let it cool in the braising liquid.
9. Fish out the sachet and discard it. Lift the meat from the sauce, pull it off the bones and throw away any sinew or tough membrane. Dig the marrow out of the bones and keep it to spread on bread.
10. Shred the meat into strands with two forks, then run a knife through them to cut 1/4- to 1/2-inch pieces.
11. Skim the fat off the braising liquid and stir the meat back in. Season with salt. Weigh out 450 g of ragu for the pasta. The rest keeps 5 days refrigerated or a month frozen.
12. Cut 624 g of tagliatelle from the egg dough.
13. Salt a large pot of water heavily and bring it to the boil.
14. Set a large saute pan over medium-low heat and add the 450 g ragu.
15. Cook the tagliatelle 30 seconds to 1 minute. It wants to be tender, not soft.
16. Move the pasta across with tongs or a pasta basket and toss about a minute to marry. A splash of pasta water loosens the sauce if it tightens. The ragu should coat the strands without clumping them.
17. Divide into bowls and finish with fennel pollen, pepper and the parmigiano.

## Notes

- Marrow and the gelatin off the bones give this braise enough richness to carry the pasta on its own. A leaner braise needs a little butter at the end to emulsify.
- Wringing the ground vegetables out in cheesecloth takes the water with it, so the soffritto softens rather than steams.
- The braise yields far more than the dish uses. 450 g of ragu dresses 624 g of tagliatelle.
- Salt the shanks 1 to 2 hours ahead at the very least, overnight if you can plan it.
- The whole 103 g of oil goes into the pot at the sear.
