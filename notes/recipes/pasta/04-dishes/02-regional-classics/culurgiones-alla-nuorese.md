# Culurgiones alla Nuorese

- Section: pasta
- Status: drafted
- Shape: culurgiones
- Filling: potato-ricotta-and-fiore-sardo-filling
- Sauce: simple-red-sauce
- Serves: 4-6
- Total time: 1 h 30 min plus the dough rest
- Region: South
- Source: Missy Robbins, Pasta

## Ingredients

- Yukon gold potatoes: 456 g
- Ricotta: 232 g
- Pecorino romano, finely grated: 22 g
- Fiore sardo, finely grated, for the filling: 30 g
- Salt: quantity not given
- Semolina dough: 1 batch
- Simple red sauce: 750 g
- Fiore sardo, finely grated, to finish: 22 g
- Mint leaves, for garnish: quantity not given

## Method

1. Cover the potatoes with cold water in a pot and set them over medium heat to come to a gentle simmer. Give them 30 to 45 minutes, until a fork slides through easily.
2. Lift them out with a slotted spoon onto a sheet pan and leave them about 15 minutes. They cool and dry out a little at the same time.
3. Once you can handle them, peel the potatoes and pass them through a ricer or food mill into a bowl.
4. Set a tamis or fine-mesh strainer over that same bowl and push the ricotta through it. Fold in the pecorino and the 30 g fiore sardo until evenly worked through. Salt until it tastes well seasoned on its own. Cover and refrigerate until you need it.
5. Make 32 culurgiones from the semolina dough and the filling.
6. Salt a large pot of water generously and bring it to the boil.
7. Warm the red sauce in a small sauce pot over low heat.
8. Cook the culurgiones 3 to 5 minutes, until tender at the thickest point of the closure.
9. While they cook, ladle 125 g of sauce into the base of each plate or shallow bowl.
10. Lift the pasta out with a spider or slotted spoon and divide it between the plates. Finish with the remaining fiore sardo and the mint leaves.

## Notes

- Mint stays out of the filling and goes on raw at the end. Cooking it shifts the flavor and dulls its impact.
- Fiore sardo works twice: smoky cheese inside the filling, and a very fine grating of it over the finished dish.
- Judge doneness at the thick seam where the pasta is folded shut, not on the flat of the parcel. That closure is the last part to come good.
- In Nuoro the filling is potato with aged pecorino, mint and sheep's milk ricotta, and the parcels come in a simple tomato sauce. Elsewhere on Sardinia the same parcels turn up in brown butter with walnuts and pistachios, or with orange zest and guanciale, or deep-fried, or pan-seared.
