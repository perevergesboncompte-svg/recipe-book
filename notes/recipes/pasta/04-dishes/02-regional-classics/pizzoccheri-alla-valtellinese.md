# Pizzoccheri alla Valtellinese

- Section: pasta
- Status: drafted
- Dough: buckwheat-dough
- Shape: pizzoccheri
- Serves: 4-6
- Total time: 1 h plus the dough rest
- Region: North
- Source: Missy Robbins, Pasta

## Ingredients

- Buckwheat dough: 1 batch
- Yukon gold potatoes, peeled and diced into ½-inch pieces: 432 g
- Olive oil: 28 g
- Unsalted butter, cold and cubed: 165 g
- Garlic, thinly sliced: 5 g
- Savoy cabbage, cored and cut into 3- to 4-inch strips: 1 kg
- Fontina, coarsely grated and at room temperature: 120 g
- Parmigiano-reggiano, finely grated: 30 g

## Method

1. Cut 454 g of pizzoccheri from the buckwheat dough.
2. Boil a small pot of heavily salted water. Cook the potatoes 3 to 5 minutes, until tender but still with a little bite. Drain and set them aside.
3. Set a large saute pan or Dutch oven over medium-low heat with the oil and 30 g of the butter. Add half the garlic and cook it 10 to 15 seconds, until it smells, with no color at all.
4. Tip in the cabbage and cook 3 to 5 minutes, until wilted and tender. Take the pan off the heat.
5. Put a large pot of water on to boil over high heat and salt it heavily.
6. In a small saucepan over medium-low heat, cook the remaining butter with the remaining garlic 3 to 4 minutes, to a foaming golden brown. Set it aside somewhere warm.
7. Cook the pizzoccheri 3 to 5 minutes, until tender without going soft.
8. Set the cabbage pan back over low heat. Lift the pasta across with a spider, add the potatoes and turn everything together gently.
9. Spread half the pasta, cabbage and potato in an even layer in a 9 by 13-inch casserole dish.
10. Scatter half the fontina and half the parmigiano over, then spoon half the warm garlic butter across the cheese.
11. Build a second layer the same way, with the rest of the pasta and cheese and the last of the garlic butter.
12. Take the casserole dish to the table and serve family style.

## Notes

- Bitto is the traditional cheese and is hard to find outside the Valtellina. Good Fontina Val d'Aosta is the stand-in.
- Grate the fontina ahead and let it come to room temperature, so it melts the moment the hot pasta lands on it.
- Do not toss the finished dish. The two layers of cheese and garlic butter are meant to melt down through the pasta as it sits.
- The traditional method boils the potatoes and cabbage first and then cooks the pasta in the same water. This version cooks the cabbage in oil and butter instead and uses separate pots.
- Buckwheat was grown in the Valtellina for centuries before this dish was recorded in the eighteenth century, because it is hardy and its growing season is short.
