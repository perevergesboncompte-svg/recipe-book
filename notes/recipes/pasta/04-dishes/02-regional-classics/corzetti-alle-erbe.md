# Corzetti alle Erbe

- Section: pasta
- Status: drafted
- Shape: corzetti
- Serves: 4-6
- Total time: 1 h plus the dough rest
- Region: North
- Source: Missy Robbins, Pasta

## Ingredients

- Egg dough: 1 batch
- Pine nuts: 60 g
- Olive oil: 42 g
- Garlic, very finely chopped: 25 g
- Unsalted butter, cold and cubed: 45 g
- Parsley, finely chopped: 20 g
- Parmigiano-reggiano, finely grated: 22 g
- Marjoram leaves: 5 g

## Method

1. Stamp 80 corzetti from the egg dough.
2. Heat the oven to 325°F. Scatter the pine nuts across a sheet pan in one layer and toast them 5 to 7 minutes to a very pale gold, looking in every couple of minutes so they do not catch. Let them cool about 15 minutes, then chop them coarsely.
3. Salt a large pot of water heavily and bring it to a boil over high heat.
4. Set a saute pan on the lowest heat you can manage. Add the garlic to the oil and cook 10 to 15 seconds, until aromatic but with no color on it. Pour in 2 ladles of pasta water (115 g), drop in the butter, swirl to emulsify, then pull the pan off the heat.
5. Cook the corzetti 1 to 2 minutes, until just tender.
6. Return the pan to low heat. Lift the pasta across with a spider or pasta basket, raise the heat to medium, and swirl 30 seconds to 1 minute to marry.
7. Off the heat, stir the parsley and half the pine nuts gently through the sauce. A splash of pasta water loosens it if it tightens.
8. Lay the pasta out on plates in a single layer. Spoon the sauce left in the pan over the top, then finish with the remaining pine nuts, the parmigiano and the marjoram.

## Notes

- Walnuts work in place of pine nuts. That is the other Ligurian reading of the sauce, with the pine nut the more usual partner.
- The marjoram goes on raw at the end rather than into the pan, which is a departure from the usual practice of cooking the herbs briefly in the sauce. Raw keeps the aroma forward.
- The garlic should get no further than aromatic. Very low heat and 10 to 15 seconds, with no color on it.
- Pine nuts turn fast. Pull them at a very light gold and check every few minutes while they are in the oven.
- The whole sauce is built in the pan while the water comes up, so nothing here needs planning ahead beyond the dough.
