# Tortelli di Zucca

- Section: pasta
- Status: drafted
- Shape: tortelli
- Filling: squash-and-mostarda-filling
- Serves: 4-6
- Total time: 3 h plus the squash drain and the dough rest
- Region: North
- Source: Missy Robbins, Pasta

## Ingredients

- Delicata squash: 900 g
- Butternut squash: 900 g
- Unsalted butter for the squash cavities, cold and cubed: 60 g
- Salt: quantity not given
- Black pepper: quantity not given
- Olive oil: 42 g
- Rosemary: 3 sprigs
- Thyme: 5 sprigs
- Garlic: 40 g
- Ricotta: 232 g
- Apple or pear mostarda, finely chopped: 30 g
- Parmigiano-reggiano for the filling, finely grated: 90 g
- Nutmeg, whole: quantity not given
- Egg dough: 1 batch
- Unsalted butter for the sauce, cold and cubed: 114 g
- Sage leaves, small: 10
- Parmigiano-reggiano to finish, finely grated: 30 g
- Amaretti cookies, finely crushed: 15 g

## Method

1. Heat the oven to 350°F.
2. Halve both squashes lengthwise and scrape out the seeds and fibers.
3. Set the halves on a sheet pan, hollow side up, and divide the 60 g of butter between the cavities. Season with salt and pepper. Drizzle the olive oil over, then strew the rosemary, thyme and garlic around them.
4. Roast 45 minutes to 1 hour, until a fork slides in easily.
5. Let the squash cool. Pull out the garlic and herbs and throw them away.
6. Scoop the flesh out of the skins and discard the skins. You are after about 450 g of flesh.
7. Stand a cheesecloth-lined chinois or fine-mesh strainer over a bowl. Spoon the flesh in, lay parchment or plastic wrap directly on the surface and press it down under a plate. The parchment is there so the squash does not stick to the plate. Refrigerate at least 2 hours, or overnight, and let the liquid seep out.
8. Pass the drained squash through a tamis or fine-mesh strainer into a bowl.
9. Pulse the ricotta in a food processor only until it turns as smooth as cake frosting, with a sheen on it. Stop there, because longer will break it. Add it to the squash.
10. Mix in the mostarda and the 90 g of parmigiano. Season with salt and pepper and about 15 gratings of nutmeg, no more, since the spice blooms once it is cooked. It should taste well seasoned. Refrigerate until you need it.
11. Form 60 tortelli from the egg dough and the filling.
12. Bring a large pot of water to the boil over high heat and salt it heavily.
13. In a small saucepan over medium heat, cook 60 g of the sauce butter 3 to 4 minutes, to just past golden brown. Drop a few sage leaves in to infuse lightly, then pull it off the heat. This gets drizzled over the plated pasta.
14. Add the tortelli to the pot and turn the heat down so the water holds a gentle simmer rather than a rolling boil. Give them 3 to 5 minutes. They are done when the thickest closure point is tender.
15. While they cook, set a large saute pan over low heat with 1 to 2 ladles of the pasta water, 55 to 115 g. Add the remaining butter and swirl to emulsify.
16. Lift the tortelli over with a spider and raise the heat to medium. Swirl 1 to 2 minutes to marry pasta and sauce, turning each piece with a spoon so every side gets coated. A splash more cooking water loosens the sauce if it tightens.
17. Divide into bowls and drizzle the reserved sage brown butter over. Finish with the 30 g of parmigiano, the crushed amaretti, the remaining sage, and grated nutmeg and pepper.

## Notes

- Cook these at a simmer, never at a boil. The filling is delicate and a hard boil breaks it out through the closure.
- Draining the roasted flesh under a weight for at least 2 hours is what keeps the filling from going watery and loose.
- Quince mostarda is the Mantuan original. Apple or pear mostarda stands in for it.
- The amaretti are a garnish here rather than a filling ingredient, which keeps the sweetness in check.
- 15 g of crushed amaretti is about 2 large cookies.
