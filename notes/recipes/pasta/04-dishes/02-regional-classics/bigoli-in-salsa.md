# Bigoli in Salsa

- Section: pasta
- Status: drafted
- Shape: bigoli
- Serves: 4-6
- Total time: 1 h 30 min plus the dough rest
- Region: North
- Source: Missy Robbins, Pasta

## Ingredients

- Whole-wheat dough: 1 batch
- Olive oil: 56 g
- Unsalted butter, cold and cubed: 45 g
- Onions, finely diced: 454 g
- Garlic, finely chopped: 15 g
- Oil-packed anchovy fillets, finely chopped: 114 g
- Water: 120 g
- Black pepper: quantity not given

## Method

1. Make 624 g of bigoli from the whole-wheat dough.
2. Put a large saute pan on low heat. Add the butter and the oil and swirl them into an emulsion.
3. Stir in the onions and give them 5 to 8 minutes to start softening.
4. Add the garlic, anchovies and water. Cook 45 minutes to 1 hour, stirring often, until the onions have caramelized to a deep golden brown. Set the pan aside.
5. Salt a large pot of water heavily and bring it to a boil over high heat.
6. Cook the bigoli 2 to 3 minutes. They want to be tender, not soft.
7. Return the saute pan to low heat. Lift the pasta across with tongs or a pasta basket, raise the heat to medium, and toss 1 to 2 minutes, feeding in about 2 ladles of pasta water (115 g) as you go. A splash more loosens the sauce if it tightens. It is ready when it clings with a gloss.
8. Divide between bowls and grind black pepper over the top.

## Notes

- The point of the long, low cook is that the anchovies and onions dissolve into something creamy. Rushing it on higher heat gives you fried onion and whole anchovy instead.
- Sardines stand in for the anchovies in some versions, notably around Mantua.
- The dough is whole-wheat, not egg. Bigoli is a thick spaghetti and the wholemeal grain is what the sauce is built against.
- The 114 g of anchovy is 20 to 24 fillets, which is less alarming than it sounds once they have melted into the onion.
- Black pepper is a late addition to the traditional version and the bite is what keeps the sweetness in check.
