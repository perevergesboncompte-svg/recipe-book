# Bucatini all’Amatriciana

- Section: pasta
- Status: drafted
- Serves: 4-6
- Total time: 40 min
- Region: Central
- Source: Missy Robbins, Pasta

## Ingredients

- Olive oil: 28 g
- Guanciale, thinly sliced: 160 g
- Tomato passata: 456 g
- Dried red chile flakes: ½ tsp
- Extruded bucatini: 624 g
- Pecorino romano: 30 g
- Pecorino romano, for garnish: 22 g

## Method

1. Set a large saute pan or Dutch oven over medium-low heat and put a paper-towel-lined plate beside it.
2. Add the oil and the guanciale. Render the fat and take the pieces just to the edge of golden without letting them brown, 5 to 8 minutes. They crisp further once they leave the pan.
3. Lift a quarter of the guanciale out with a slotted spoon and drain it on the paper towels. Those pieces are the garnish.
4. Drop the heat to low. Add the passata and the chile flakes and cook 8 to 10 minutes, until the raw edge of the tomato is gone and the flavors have knitted together. You are not trying to reduce the sauce.
5. Salt a large pot of water generously and bring it to the boil.
6. Cook the bucatini 5 to 8 minutes, to al dente.
7. Move the pasta across with tongs or a pasta basket, add a splash of pasta water and toss 1 to 2 minutes to marry. Take the pan off the heat and toss 30 g of the pecorino through. If the sauce feels tight, loosen it with more pasta water and keep tossing. It is married when it clings with a glossy sheen.
8. Divide into bowls and finish with the remaining pecorino and the reserved guanciale.

## Notes

- No onion and no garlic here. In Amatrice the sauce is tomato, pecorino and guanciale, with chile flakes at most.
- The dish takes more heat than the ½ tsp of chile given, so scale the chile up if you want it hotter.
- Pulling a quarter of the guanciale out before the tomato goes in keeps those pieces crisp for the top. The rest softens into the sauce.
- The passata only cooks long enough to lose its rawness. Reducing it tips the balance away from the pecorino and guanciale.
