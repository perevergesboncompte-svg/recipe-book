# Cjalsons

- Section: pasta
- Status: drafted
- Shape: cjalsons
- Filling: ricotta-apple-and-dried-fruit-filling
- Serves: 4-6
- Total time: 2 h plus the dough rest
- Region: North
- Source: Missy Robbins, Pasta

## Ingredients

- Ricotta: 348 g
- Smoked ricotta for the filling, coarsely grated: 57 g
- Granny Smith apple, peeled and finely grated: 64 g
- Dried pear, finely chopped: 29 g
- Golden raisins, finely chopped: 15 g
- Montasio cheese, finely grated: 28 g
- Orange peel, pith removed and finely chopped: 1 orange
- Lemon peel, pith removed and finely chopped: 1 lemon
- Cinnamon for the filling, freshly ground: 1/4 tsp
- Natural cocoa powder for the filling: 1/2 tsp
- Salt: quantity not given
- Black pepper: quantity not given
- Egg dough: 1 batch
- Unsalted butter, cold and cubed: 150 g
- Cinnamon stick to finish, grated, or ground cinnamon: 1 stick
- Natural cocoa powder to finish: quantity not given
- Smoked ricotta to finish, in one piece: 114 g

## Method

1. Pass the ricotta through a tamis or a fine-mesh strainer into a bowl.
2. Fold in the smoked ricotta, apple, pear, raisins, Montasio, both citrus peels, the 1/4 tsp cinnamon and the 1/2 tsp cocoa until the mixture is even.
3. Season with salt and black pepper. Aim for properly seasoned. Cover it and refrigerate until you need it.
4. Make 48 to 60 cjalsons from the egg dough and the filling.
5. Salt a large pot of water heavily and bring it to a boil over high heat.
6. Set a small saucepan over medium heat and cook 114 g of the butter 3 to 4 minutes, to just past golden brown. Take it off the heat. This brown butter goes over the plates at the end.
7. Drop the cjalsons in and bring the water down to a gentle simmer rather than a rolling boil, since the filling can break over high heat. Cook 2 to 3 minutes. They are done when the thickest seal has gone tender.
8. While they cook, set a large saute pan over medium heat with 2 ladles of pasta water (115 g) and the remaining butter, and swirl them together to emulsify.
9. Lift the pasta across with a spider or pasta basket and swirl about 30 seconds, turning the pieces with a spoon so all sides are coated.
10. Plate the pasta and spoon brown butter over each serving. Dust with cinnamon, then with the barest fall of cocoa, sifted through a fine sieve, a baker's wand or a shaker. Grate the 114 g piece of smoked ricotta over the top and finish with black pepper.

## Notes

- Simmer rather than boil. The filling is soft and the seams open if the water moves hard.
- The 150 g of butter splits in two. 114 g browns in a small pan for drizzling and the remainder emulsifies with pasta water into the pan sauce.
- Cocoa goes on in a trace through a strainer. A little travels a very long way here.
- Sweet versions of the dish lean on dried fruit, chocolate, ricotta and biscotti. Savory ones lean on foraged herbs, ricotta, nuts and spices. This one sits between the two.
- The spelling moves around the Carnic Alps along with the recipe. Cjarsons and cialzons name the same pasta.
