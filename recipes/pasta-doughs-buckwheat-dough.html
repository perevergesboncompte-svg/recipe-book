Buckwheat Dough

Makes
715 g
Active Time
8-10 min kneading
Rest
30 min minimum, wrapped
Source
Pasta, Missy Robbins

Print card (PDF)

Ingredients

Batch
  • Tipo 00 flour: 300 g
  • Buckwheat flour: 75 g
  • Egg yolks: 340 g
  • Extra 00 flour for kneading: as needed

Method

  1. Stir the 00 and buckwheat flours together in a bowl until the colour is even throughout.
  2. Tip the mixture onto a wooden surface and bank it into a wall around a well 5 to 6 inches across, no wider. Too much space and the wall will not hold the yolks once you start drawing flour in.
  3. Mix the yolks, but do not beat them. Beaten yolks run and are harder to contain.
  4. Pour half the yolks into the well. With a fork, pull flour down from the inner face of the wall, working round the circumference in one continuous direction.
  5. Add the rest in the same way, taking in flour as you go. If the wall gives way, catch the escape with a bench scraper and fold it back in at each edge until the mass is thick enough to hold itself.
  6. Knead 8 to 10 minutes. Press down and away with the heel of your hand, using your weight rather than your arm, hold the near edge with the other hand, then rotate 180 degrees, fold and press again. The dough starts sticky, so keep returning what clings to your hands.
  7. Scrape clumps off the board as you work. Dust with flour only if you must, since extra flour dries it out.
  8. Stop when it is smooth and firm and springs back slightly when poked. Play-Doh is the texture to aim for.
  9. Wrap and rest at least 30 minutes, which is what makes it pliable enough to roll.

Notes

  • 340 g of yolks is roughly 18 to 20 eggs, so decide what the whites are for before you start.
  • Buckwheat carries no gluten, so the 300 g of 00 is doing all the structural work. The ratio is not something to shift.
  • Chilled overnight is fine. Pull it out 20 minutes ahead so it comes back to room temperature before rolling.
  • Use within 24 hours.
  • This is the dough for pizzoccheri.

Preserving

Make the full batch and freeze whatever you will not roll out. Refrigerated dough is good for about a day, so freezing is the answer beyond that rather than stretching it.

  • Divide into meal-sized portions before freezing, so you thaw only what you need.
  • Flatten each portion into a disc rather than a ball. It freezes and thaws evenly that way.
  • Wrap tight in plastic, then bag it, or it dries and picks up freezer burn.
  • Thaw overnight in the fridge, then 20 minutes at room temperature before rolling.
  • Do not refreeze thawed dough.
