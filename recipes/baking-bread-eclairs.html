Baking / Bread

Éclairs

Filling
Pastry Cream
Makes
12 éclairs
Active Time
45 min
Total Time
about 2 h, with the pastry cream already made and cold
Oven
425F / 220C for 10 min, then 375F / 190C for 12 min
Source
Tartine, A Classic Revisited

Print card (PDF)

Ingredients

Batch
  • Nonfat milk: 120 ml
  • Water: 120 ml
  • Unsalted butter: 115 g
  • Sugar: 1 tsp
  • Salt: 0.25 tsp
  • All-purpose flour: 140 g
  • Eggs, large: 5
  • Bittersweet chocolate, coarsely chopped: 115 g
  • Heavy cream, for the glaze: 120 ml
  • Light corn syrup: 1 tbsp
  • Pastry cream, cold: 300 ml

Method

  1. Make the pastry cream in advance and chill it hard. It has to be properly cold to pipe.
  2. Heat the oven to 425F / 220C. Butter a baking sheet, or line one with parchment paper.
  3. Combine the milk, water, butter, sugar and salt in a heavy saucepan over medium heat. Bring it to a rolling boil, by which point the butter will have melted away.
  4. Add all the flour in one go and beat it hard with a wooden spoon. Carry on about 3 minutes, until the paste pulls together into a smooth mass, lets go of the pan sides, and has dried out a little.
  5. Scrape it into a stand mixer bowl fitted with the paddle, or a heatproof bowl to work by hand.
  6. On medium-high speed, beat in the eggs one at a time, each fully absorbed before the next one goes in. The finished paste is thick, smooth and shiny. A wooden spoon handles it the same way.
  7. Load a piping bag fitted with a 12 mm plain tip, no. 6 or 7, putting in only as much paste as is comfortable to handle.
  8. Pipe fingers 12 cm long and 2.5 cm wide, spaced about 5 cm apart. Should a stroke finish with a bulge or a tail, flatten it away with a wet fingertip.
  9. Bake about 10 minutes, until they have puffed and are just beginning to colour.
  10. Take the oven down to 375F / 190C and give them about 12 minutes more, until the shells are browned all over, hollow inside, and surprisingly light when you pick one up.
  11. Out of the oven, drive a metal skewer into the end of each shell to open a small vent for the steam. Skip this and the shells cave in. Cool them on wire racks.
  12. Make the glaze. Put the chopped chocolate and the corn syrup, which is there for shine, into a heatproof bowl. Bring the cream up in a small pan until it is just about to boil, then pour it over. Leave it alone for about 2 minutes while the chocolate melts, then stir gently with a rubber spatula, working in as little air as you can, until it is smooth and glossy. Let it cool to barely warm.
  13. Stir the cold pastry cream smooth and load it into a piping bag with a plain tip that has a very small opening.
  14. Fill each shell through the vent hole until it feels heavy in the hand. Open a second hole at the other end and fill from both ends when the cream will not travel the length of a shell, which happens where pockets have formed inside.
  15. Take each filled éclair and dip its top into the glaze, shake it gently to let the excess run off, and stand it the right way up on a wire rack until the glaze firms.
  16. Serve them immediately, or refrigerate for up to 6 hours.

Notes

  • Fill the shells as soon as they are cool enough to handle, and eat them the day they are filled.
  • Every bump and wobble in the piping is exaggerated by the bake, so a good shell starts with good piping. The paste is forgiving — scoop up a bad finger and pipe it again, and smooth small bumps away with a dampened fingertip.
  • There is a split-and-fill route as well. Halve the cooled shells with a serrated knife, dip the top halves in the glaze and leave them on a rack to set, spoon pastry cream into the bottoms, then put the glazed tops back on.
  • Unfilled shells freeze wrapped tightly for up to 3 weeks. Crisp them straight from the freezer at 450F / 230C for about 10 minutes, then cool them before filling.
  • Matcha pastry cream works in place of the plain, and a matcha glaze in place of the chocolate.
