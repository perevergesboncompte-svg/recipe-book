Caramelle with Caramelized Onion, Brown Butter, and Balsamic

Filling
Caramelized Onion and Parmigiano Filling
Shape
Caramelle
Serves
6-8
Total Time
5 h plus the dough rest
Source
Missy Robbins, Pasta

Print card (PDF)

Ingredients

Batch
  • Country bread, torn into pieces: 228 g
  • Olive oil, for the bread crumbs: 56 g
  • Garlic: 10 g
  • Olive oil, for the onions: 125 g
  • Unsalted butter, cold and cubed, for the onions: 60 g
  • Yellow onions, thinly sliced: 1.8 kg
  • Water: 237 g
  • Rosemary, for the onions: 2 sprigs
  • Thyme, for the onions: 2 sprigs
  • Parmigiano-reggiano, finely grated, for the filling: 60 g
  • Salt: quantity not given
  • Black pepper: quantity not given
  • Egg dough: 1 batch
  • Unsalted butter, cold and cubed, to finish: 150 g
  • Aged balsamic vinegar: quantity not given
  • Rosemary, leaves picked, to garnish: 1 sprig
  • Thyme, leaves picked, to garnish: 2 sprigs
  • Parmigiano-reggiano, finely grated, to garnish: 22 g

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 200°F.
  2. Spread the torn bread over a sheet pan and dry it out in the oven for 30 to 45 minutes. It should take no color at all. Crush a shard with the back of a spoon to check, the inside has to be bone dry. Let it cool.
  3. Pulse the cooled bread in a food processor down to fine crumbs, stopping while they still have some texture rather than turning to dust.
  4. Set a saute pan over medium-low heat with the 56 g olive oil and the garlic. Cook 1 to 2 minutes, until the garlic scents the oil, then lift the garlic out and keep it for another use.
  5. Drop the heat to low, add the crumbs and stir now and then until golden brown, 5 to 8 minutes. Drain them on a paper-towel-lined plate and let them cool.
  6. Weigh out 104 g of crumbs for the filling. The rest keeps in an airtight container somewhere cool and dry for a week.
  7. Warm the 125 g oil and the 60 g butter together in a large Dutch oven set over low heat, swirling them into each other.
  8. Add the onions and turn them so every slice carries the fat. Cook 10 to 15 minutes until soft and translucent, stirring occasionally so nothing catches.
  9. Pour in the water. Tie the 2 rosemary and 2 thyme sprigs into a bundle with kitchen twine and bury it in the pot.
  10. Lay a sheet of parchment directly on the onions and cook 2 to 3 hours, lifting the paper to stir from time to time, until they have collapsed completely and gone deep brown. By then the water should all have evaporated. Take the pot off the heat and let it cool.
  11. Blend the cooled onions smooth in a high-speed blender.
  12. Scrape the puree into a bowl, fold in the 104 g bread crumbs and the 60 g parmigiano, and season with salt and pepper. Cover and refrigerate until you shape the pasta.
  13. Make 80 to 100 caramelle from the egg dough and the filling.
  14. Salt a large pot of water heavily and bring it to the boil.
  15. In a small saucepan over medium heat, cook 85 g of the butter for 3 to 4 minutes, just past golden brown. Set it aside to drizzle over the plated pasta.
  16. Add the caramelle and pull the heat back so the water holds a gentle simmer rather than a boil. The filling is delicate and a hard boil will split it out. Give them 2 to 3 minutes, until the thickest closure point is tender.
  17. Meanwhile set a large saute pan over low heat with the remaining 65 g butter and 2 to 3 ladles of pasta water, 115 g to 170 g. Swirl to emulsify.
  18. Lift the pasta across with a spider, raise the heat to medium and swirl 30 seconds to 1 minute, turning the pieces with a spoon so all sides are coated. A splash more pasta water loosens the sauce if it tightens.
  19. Transfer to plates or a platter and spoon the pan sauce over, slackening it first with a little pasta water if it needs it. Drizzle with the balsamic and the reserved brown butter, then finish with the rosemary, thyme, parmigiano and pepper.

Notes

  • The balsamic has to be an aged bottle with sweetness and some viscosity, not the thin kind you would whisk into a vinaigrette. It does not need to be twenty-five years old.
  • Bread crumbs and parmigiano are in the filling for binding and creaminess, not for flavor.
  • The crumb batch is deliberately larger than the filling needs. 104 g goes in, the surplus keeps a week.
  • The 150 g of finishing butter splits two ways: 85 g browned for drizzling, 65 g for the pan sauce.
  • Caramelle go into water at a bare simmer. A rolling boil breaks the filling out of the closures.
