Pasta / Shapes

Strangozzi

Dough
Egg Dough
Rest
30-40 min at room temperature
Source
Pasta, Missy Robbins
Formed
strand
Region
Umbria, chiefly around Spoleto, where it is also called stringozzi

Method

  1. Make an egg-based dough, then roll and sheet it.
  2. Give a wooden work surface a light dusting of 00 flour. Line a sheet pan with parchment, then dust the parchment with semolina.
  3. Knife the sheets into 9-inch lengths and trim the ragged edges off. Save the trim for soup. Keep the sheets under plastic or a towel.
  4. Fit the fettuccine cutter to a hand-crank machine or the KitchenAid attachment.
  5. Hold one end of a sheet, feed the other end into the cutter, and crank, keeping the sheet nearly perpendicular to the rollers. Catch the strands underneath with your free hand.
  6. Dust the strands generously with 00 flour and shake them apart. Bend the batch into a horseshoe and set it on the pan.
  7. Repeat with the rest of the sheets.
  8. Dry 30 to 40 minutes at room temperature.
  9. To hold, cover the pan with plastic and refrigerate up to 24 hours.

Notes

  • Cut on the fettuccine cutter. The book gives no strand width for this shape.
  • Sheets are 9 inches long, shorter than the 18 inches used for most of the nest-coiled ribbons.
  • Drying runs 30 to 40 minutes, longer than the 20 to 30 most hand-cut ribbons get.
  • Shaped into a horseshoe rather than a nest.
  • A thicker, sturdier fettuccine. The extra body is what lets it carry heavy sauces.
  • Refrigerated shelf life is 24 hours.
  • Best with black truffle in a sauce of garlic, anchovy, and olive oil. Also good with a peppery tomato sauce alla spoletina, with meat ragu, or alla norcina.
