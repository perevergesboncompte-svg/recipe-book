Pasta / Dishes

Orecchiette con Cime di Rapa

Shape
Orecchiette
Region
South
Serves
4-6
Total Time
1 h 30 min plus the dough rest
Source
Missy Robbins, Pasta

Print card (PDF)

Ingredients

Batch
  • Country bread, half a loaf, torn into pieces: 228 g
  • Olive oil, for the bread crumbs: 56 g
  • Garlic cloves, for the bread crumbs: 10 g
  • Semolina dough: 1 batch
  • Broccoli rabe, one large bunch: 346 g
  • Olive oil: 42 g
  • Garlic, finely chopped: 45 g
  • Oil-packed anchovy fillets, finely chopped: 30 g
  • Dried red chile flakes: 4 g
  • Dry white wine: 110 g
  • Pecorino romano, finely grated: 22 g

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 200°F for the bread crumbs.
  2. Spread the torn bread over a sheet pan and dry it out in the oven, 30 to 45 minutes, without letting it take any color. Crush a shard with the back of a spoon to check — the inside should be completely dry. Let it cool.
  3. Pulse the cooled bread in a food processor down to fine crumbs. Stop while they still hold some texture rather than carrying on to dust.
  4. Set a saute pan over medium-low heat with the 56 g olive oil and the whole garlic cloves. Cook 1 to 2 minutes, until the garlic has perfumed the oil, then fish the cloves out and keep them for another use.
  5. Turn the heat to low and line a plate with paper towels. Add the crumbs and cook, stirring now and then, until golden brown, 5 to 8 minutes. Drain them on the plate and let them cool.
  6. Measure out 40 g of the crumbs for this dish. The rest keeps a week in an airtight container somewhere cool and dry.
  7. Make 624 g of orecchiette from the semolina dough.
  8. Strip the broccoli rabe leaves from the stems. Chop the stems and the florets into very small pieces. Chop the leaves fine and keep them to one side.
  9. Salt a large pot of water generously and bring it to the boil.
  10. Set a large saute pan over low heat. Add the 42 g olive oil and the chopped garlic and cook gently 10 to 15 seconds, taking no color.
  11. Stir in the anchovies, chile flakes and the broccoli rabe stems and florets. Add the wine and about 3 ladles of pasta water, 170 g. Braise 3 to 5 minutes, until tender but still bright green. Add the leaves and give them 1 to 2 minutes, then set the pan aside.
  12. Cook the orecchiette 4 to 6 minutes, to al dente.
  13. Lift the pasta into the saute pan with a spider or pasta basket and raise the heat to medium. Toss gently 1 to 2 minutes to marry. A splash of pasta water loosens the sauce if it feels tight.
  14. Divide into bowls and finish with the bread crumbs and pecorino.

Notes

  • Cook the broccoli rabe right down until it collapses into something closer to a ragù alongside the anchovy and garlic. That breakdown is what makes the dish — bitter, saline and rich enough to stand in for meat.
  • Stems, florets and leaves get chopped and cooked separately. The leaves go in last and need only 1 to 2 minutes.
  • Dry the bread at 200°F with no color on it, then toast the crumbs separately in garlic oil. Pulse them short so they keep some grit.
  • No sausage. That arrived with the dish in the United States and the original is complex enough without it.
  • Broccoli rabe is a cool-weather crop, which sets the season for this one.
