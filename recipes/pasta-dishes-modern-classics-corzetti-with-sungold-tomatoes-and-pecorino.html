Pasta / Dishes

Corzetti with Sungold Tomatoes, Pecorino, and Herbs

Shape
Corzetti
Serves
4-6
Total Time
1 h plus the dough rest
Source
Missy Robbins, Pasta

Print card (PDF)

Ingredients

Batch
  • Sungold tomatoes: 500 g
  • Olive oil: 56 g
  • Garlic, finely chopped: 40 g
  • Salt: quantity not given
  • Egg dough: 1 batch
  • Unsalted butter, cold and cubed: 30 g
  • Pecorino romano, finely grated: 22 g
  • Chervil, tender leaves: 5 g
  • Basil, small to medium leaves: 5 g
  • Marjoram leaves: 5 g

Method

  1. Bring a large pot of water to the boil and set a colander inside an ice-water bath.
  2. Nick a small X in the base of each tomato with a sharp paring knife. Drop them into the boiling water for 5 to 10 seconds to loosen the skins, lift them into the ice bath with a slotted spoon, and after 30 seconds move them to a bowl.
  3. Peel the tomatoes over a second bowl so you catch whatever juice runs out. Throw the skins away.
  4. Put the olive oil and garlic into the bowl of tomato juice, then the peeled tomatoes and salt, and stir it all together. Set aside.
  5. Stamp 80 corzetti from the egg dough.
  6. Salt a large pot of water heavily and bring it to the boil.
  7. Cook the corzetti 1 to 2 minutes. They want to be tender, not soft.
  8. While they boil, tip the whole contents of the tomato bowl into a large saute pan over low heat. Cook 1 to 2 minutes, crushing the tomatoes lightly with the back of a spoon so they give up their juice.
  9. Add the butter and one ladle of pasta water, about 55 g, and swirl to emulsify.
  10. Lift the pasta across with a spider, raise the heat to medium and swirl 1 to 2 minutes to marry, turning the corzetti with a spoon so every face gets coated. More pasta water loosens the sauce if it tightens. It is ready when it clings with a gloss.
  11. Plate in a single layer and spoon the sauce left in the pan over each portion. Garnish with the pecorino, then the chervil, basil and marjoram.

Notes

  • Peeling the tomatoes is the technique the dish turns on. Without their skins they absorb the marinade and then the sauce, instead of holding both out.
  • Sungolds are a high-summer tomato, which fixes the window for this one.
  • The tomatoes barely cook, 1 to 2 minutes, only long enough to release juice.
  • Peel over a bowl. The juice you catch is the base of the marinade.
