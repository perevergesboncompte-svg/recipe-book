Pasta / Dishes

Strangozzi alla Norcina

Shape
Strangozzi
Region
Central
Serves
4-6
Total Time
45 min plus the dough rest
Source
Missy Robbins, Pasta

Print card (PDF)

Ingredients

Batch
  • Oil-packed anchovy fillets, coarsely chopped: 40 g
  • Garlic, roughly chopped: 5 g
  • Fresh black truffle, coarsely chopped: 60 g
  • Water: 30-60 g
  • Olive oil: 42 g
  • Egg dough: 1 batch
  • Unsalted butter, cold and cubed: 30 g
  • Fresh black truffle, for shaving, optional: quantity not given

Method

  1. Put the anchovies, garlic, truffle and 30 g of the water into a high-speed blender. You want only enough water to get the blades to catch. Puree to a fine paste.
  2. Keep the blender on high and pour the olive oil in gradually until the paste is well emulsified. Set it aside.
  3. Make 624 g of strangozzi from the egg dough.
  4. Salt a large pot of water generously and bring it to a rolling boil.
  5. Cook the strangozzi 3 to 5 minutes, to tender. Being a thick pasta it keeps some bite rather than going soft the way fettuccine does.
  6. While it boils, set a large saute pan over low heat. Stir the truffle-anchovy paste together with 2 to 3 ladles of pasta water, 115 g to 170 g.
  7. Lift the pasta across with tongs or a pasta basket and raise the heat to medium. Toss 1 to 2 minutes to marry it to the sauce.
  8. Add the butter and keep tossing. A splash of pasta water loosens the sauce if it tightens. It is married when it clings with a glossy sheen.
  9. Divide between bowls. Shave fresh truffle over the top if you have any to spare.

Notes

  • Two different dishes carry this name. This is the one built on black truffle, anchovy and garlic pounded to a paste, not the sausage-and-heavy-cream version that dominates the search results.
  • Preserved black truffles will stand in for fresh, though quality varies drastically between brands. Savini sliced summer truffles in oil are a reliable option.
  • Fresh winter and summer black truffles are seasonal and come from specialty purveyors outside Italy. Around Norcia in Umbria they are everyday cooking, used as freely as garlic.
  • Start the paste with 30 g of water and add more only if the blender will not pull the mixture down. Too much water and the oil will not emulsify properly.
