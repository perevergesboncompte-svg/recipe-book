Pasta / Dishes

Busiate alla Pesto Trapanese

Shape
Busiate
Region
South
Serves
4-6
Total Time
1 h plus the dough rest
Source
Missy Robbins, Pasta

Print card (PDF)

Ingredients

Batch
  • Cherry tomatoes: 545 g
  • Garlic cloves: 10 g
  • Basil leaves: 10
  • Sicilian almonds, coarsely chopped: 100 g
  • Olive oil, plus more to cover: 75 g
  • Pecorino romano: 68 g
  • Salt: quantity not given
  • Semolina dough: 1 batch

Method

  1. Bring a small pot of water to the boil over high heat. Set up an ice-water bath with a colander sitting in it.
  2. Score an X into the base of each tomato with a sharp paring knife. Drop them into the boiling water for 5 to 10 seconds to loosen the skins, then move them to the ice bath with a slotted spoon. After 30 seconds, lift them into a bowl, slip the skins off and discard them. Lay the tomatoes on a kitchen towel to shed any water they are still carrying.
  3. Tip the tomatoes into a high-speed blender. Add the garlic and pulse gently to a puree. Add the basil and keep going, then the almonds, blending to a thick, uniform paste.
  4. With the blender on low, drizzle in the 75 g of oil. Once it is smooth, scrape it into a mixing bowl and fold the cheese through until well combined. Taste and add salt.
  5. Measure out 383 g of the pesto for this dish. Put the rest in an airtight container under a film of olive oil and refrigerate up to 3 days.
  6. Make 624 g of busiate from the semolina dough.
  7. Salt a large pot of water generously and bring it to the boil.
  8. Cook the busiate 3 to 5 minutes, to al dente.
  9. Put the measured pesto in a large mixing bowl. Lift the pasta straight into it with a spider or pasta basket. Toss gently and feed in pasta water 15 g at a time until the pesto turns creamy and coats every strand.
  10. Divide into bowls.

Notes

  • The pesto never goes near the heat. It is married to the pasta in a bowl, loosened with pasta water a tablespoon at a time.
  • Pesto alla trapanese is Genoese pesto rebuilt on Sicilian ingredients. Almonds take the place of pine nuts, ripe tomato pushes basil into the background, and some versions work mint in.
  • A blender or food processor gives the smooth, Genoese-like paste this recipe aims at. By mortar and pestle you can stop wherever you like, and the traditional texture is rougher than this.
  • Whether to put cheese in at all is contested. This version folds in 68 g pecorino romano.
  • The recipe makes more pesto than the dish needs. Keep the surplus sealed under oil.
