Empanadas / Fillings

Relleno de Atún en Lata

Yield
filling for 1 large empanada
Active Time
15 min
Rest
30 min to cool
Source
El Comidista style, adapted from the zamburiñas empanada, classic Galician empanada
Cook
30 min

Print card (PDF)

Ingredients

Batch
  • Canned tuna, drained, in olive oil (5-6 tins): 350-400 g
  • Onions: 2-3
  • Red pepper (or ½ red + ½ green): 1
  • Garlic cloves: 2
  • Ripe tomato, grated (or 2 tbsp tomate frito): 1
  • Sweet pimentón: 1 tsp
  • Hard-boiled eggs: 2
  • Green olives, pitted (optional): 50-75 g
  • Dry white wine: 50-80 ml
  • Parsley: to taste
  • Salt: to taste
  • Olive oil: to taste

Method

  1. Drain the tuna well, keeping some of its oil to boost the sofrito. Boil the eggs about 10 min, peel and chop or slice them. Finely chop the onion, garlic and pepper, and grate the tomato.
  2. Heat plenty of olive oil, colour the garlic, then add the onion with a pinch of salt and sweat it 15 to 20 min over medium-low heat until meltingly soft.
  3. Add the red pepper and cook another 10 to 15 min until it softens.
  4. Lower the heat, add the pimentón and stir for 20 seconds so it does not burn. Add the tomato and cook until its water is gone.
  5. Raise the heat, add the white wine and reduce until the zaragallada is compact and dry.
  6. Off the heat, fold in the flaked tuna and the parsley using residual heat only, without cooking it. Check the salt.
  7. Cool and drain the excess oil. Scatter the boiled egg and the olives when assembling, before the tapa goes on.

Notes

  • Must be reduced dry and cooled: "zaragallada seca y fría", because excess liquid softens the base and splits the empanada. Drain it in a sieve.
  • The tuna goes in last and uncooked, since it is already cooked. Cook it again and it dries out and falls apart.
  • The fish is pushed up to around 400 g so the tuna to sofrito ratio lands near 1 : 1. The zamburiñas version it was adapted from came out short of filling.
  • Keep the pimentón off high heat. Burnt, it turns the whole filling bitter.
  • Boiled egg and olives go in at assembly rather than into the sofrito, so they stay distributed and hold their shape.
  • Use the tuna's oil in the sofrito and for brushing the dough, which browns nicely.
