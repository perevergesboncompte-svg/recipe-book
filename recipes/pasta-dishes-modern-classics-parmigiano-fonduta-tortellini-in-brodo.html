Parmigiano Fonduta-Filled Tortellini in Brodo

Filling
Parmigiano Fonduta Filling
Shape
Tortellini
Serves
6-8
Total Time
5 h plus the dough rest
Source
Missy Robbins, Pasta

Print card (PDF)

Ingredients

Batch
  • Egg dough: 1 batch
  • Heavy cream: 474 g
  • Parmigiano-reggiano, finely grated, for the filling: 225 g
  • Egg yolks, stirred: 27 g
  • Chicken, whole: 1.6-1.8 kg
  • Water: 2.8-4.7 kg
  • Carrots, peeled and cut into 1-inch pieces: 300 g
  • Onion, quartered: 370 g
  • Celery, cut into 1-inch pieces: 227 g
  • Leek, cut into 1-inch pieces: 150 g
  • Parsley: 30 g
  • Garlic, head halved: 100 g
  • Black peppercorns: 9 g
  • Fresh bay leaf: 1
  • Parmigiano-reggiano, finely grated, to finish: 45 g
  • Salt: quantity not given
  • Olive oil: quantity not given
  • Black pepper: quantity not given

Method

  1. For the filling, bring the cream to a bare simmer in a saucepan over medium heat. Drop the heat to low and hold it there until the cream has reduced by a third, 1 to 1½ hours. Watch the bottom of the pan, it scorches easily. Let it stand 5 minutes.
  2. Pour the cream into a high-speed blender. Run it on low to medium and rain the parmigiano in slowly, until the mixture emulsifies. Taste before you salt, because the cheese brings a lot of salinity already.
  3. With the blender still turning, trickle the yolks in so they temper instead of splitting the mixture. Cover and refrigerate at least 1 hour. If it sits several hours or overnight, pull it out 30 minutes before you pipe so it softens.
  4. For the brodo, put the chicken in a large stockpot with 2.8 kg of the water, adding more if the bird is not covered. Bring it up to a simmer slowly over low heat, 45 minutes to 1 hour. Scum rises once it simmers. Skim it off continuously with a big spoon, which is what keeps the brodo clear.
  5. After an hour, top the pot up with the remaining water if the level has dropped below the chicken, then simmer 2 hours more, still skimming and still topping up as needed.
  6. Drop in the carrots, onion, celery, leek, garlic, parsley, peppercorns and bay leaf. Hold it at a slow simmer another 1 to 2 hours, until it tastes full.
  7. Line a fine-mesh strainer with cheesecloth, or with a coffee filter, and pass the brodo through it. Set 1.6 kg aside for this dish. Cool the rest and keep it in an airtight container, 5 days refrigerated or a month frozen. Save the chicken meat too.
  8. Make 90 to 120 tortellini with the egg dough and the fonduta.
  9. Salt a large pot of water heavily and bring it to the boil over high heat.
  10. While the water heats, warm the measured brodo in a saucepan over low heat and salt it to taste.
  11. Slide the tortellini into the water and pull the heat back to a gentle simmer. A rolling boil splits this filling. Cook 1 to 2 minutes. The thickest closure should give tenderly and the fonduta inside should have gone molten and creamy.
  12. Lift them out with a spider or pasta basket into bowls, ladle the brodo over and finish with a drizzle of oil, pepper to taste and the parmigiano.

Notes

  • The fonduta is parmigiano, cream and enough yolk to bind, with no wine and no flour. It is piped cold and melts in the pot to the texture of a runny yolk.
  • Skim the brodo the whole way through. Clarity is the point of the dish.
  • Add the yolks in a thin stream with the blender running. Dumped in, they break the emulsion.
  • Filling that has been refrigerated overnight needs half an hour at room temperature before it will pipe.
  • Late in autumn this gets white truffle shaved over the top, but it stands up without.
