Neapolitan-Style Pizza in a Home Oven

Dough
High-Hydration Pizza Dough for a Home Oven
Makes
3 pizzas
Active Time
15 min
Oven
maximum, around 250C or 480F, top and bottom heat, 45 min preheat
Source
opelouro, youtube.com/@opelouro
Energy Cost
$2.27 (45 min preheat plus 21 min baking for three pizzas, 3.5 kW at 59 cents/kWh)

Print card (PDF)

Ingredients

Batch
  • Pizza dough: 3 balls of 260 g
  • Tomato passata: 2 0.5 tsp per pizza
  • Fresh mozzarella, well drained: quantity not given
  • Pepperoni: quantity not given
  • Fresh basil: quantity not given
  • Parmigiano-reggiano, grated: quantity not given
  • Semolina, for dusting: quantity not given
  • Salt: quantity not given
  • Olive oil: quantity not given

Method

  1. Turn an oven tray upside down so you have a flat surface to launch from, and set the oven to its maximum with heat top and bottom. Leave the tray in there at least 45 minutes.
  2. Drain the mozzarella while the oven heats. It gives up a surprising amount of water, and that water ends up on the pizza otherwise.
  3. Dust a ball generously with semolina and open it out on the bench, pressing from the centre with your fingertips held perpendicular to the dough. It is extensible enough to give easily, so work gently.
  4. Move it onto a peel, or onto a piece of stiff cardboard if you have no peel, and shake the loose semolina off.
  5. Spread about two and a half teaspoons of passata and no more, then a pinch of salt and a little oil. Nothing else goes on yet.
  6. Slide it onto the hot tray and bake around 4 minutes. The base wants to be cooked but not coloured. Lift an edge at 3 minutes and pull it while it is still soft enough to bend.
  7. Lay the basil, the drained mozzarella and the pepperoni over the part-baked base.
  8. Send it back for 2 to 3 minutes, 4 at the outside. If your oven has a grill, this is the stage to use it, to finish the top.
  9. Off the tray, finish with grated parmigiano and a last drizzle of olive oil.

Notes

  • Splitting the bake is the whole trick. One continuous bake in a home oven dries the base to a cracker before the top is anywhere near done, so the base goes in nearly bare and the toppings get a short second pass.
  • Pull the first bake while the base still bends. That is what leaves the finished pizza soft instead of hard.
  • The tray goes in upside down for a flat launch surface. The right way up it has a lip and a dip, and sliding a wet pizza off a peel onto that is awkward.
  • No stone and no steel here. The preheated tray is all the thermal mass there is, which is why it needs the full 45 minutes rather than the 10 a stone would want.
  • Keep the passata sparing, and every topping in its own measure rather than piled on. A wet pizza will not set in a home oven.
  • A home oven stops around 250C against the 450C-plus a Neapolitan base is built for. Everything odd about this recipe, the high hydration, the sugar, the oil, the two bakes, exists to work around that ceiling.
