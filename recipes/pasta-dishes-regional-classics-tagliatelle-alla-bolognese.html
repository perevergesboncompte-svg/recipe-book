Pasta / Dishes

Tagliatelle alla Bolognese

Shape
Tagliatelle
Region
North
Serves
4-6
Total Time
4 h plus the dough rest
Source
Missy Robbins, Pasta

Print card (PDF)

Ingredients

Batch
  • Pancetta, in one slab: 170 g
  • Onion: 370 g
  • Carrot, peeled: 150 g
  • Celery: 150 g
  • Ground beef: 454 g
  • Ground pork: 454 g
  • Ground veal: 454 g
  • Olive oil: 56 g
  • Tomato paste: 130 g
  • Dry white wine: 220 g
  • Water: 474 g
  • Milk: 237 g
  • Egg dough: 1 batch
  • Unsalted butter, cold and cubed: 30 g
  • Parmigiano-reggiano, finely grated: 45 g

Method

  1. Chill the meat grinder and its small die in the refrigerator at least 1 hour, or 30 minutes in the freezer.
  2. Fit the small die and run the pancetta through twice. Set it aside.
  3. Cut the onion, carrot and celery into pieces 1/2 to 1 inch across and grind all three. Gather the ground vegetables in cheesecloth and wring the liquid out. That is the soffritto.
  4. Set a large heavy saucepot or Dutch oven over low heat and render the pancetta 5 to 8 minutes. It should be cooked through but neither browned nor crisp.
  5. Add the beef, pork and veal, raise the heat to medium-high, and saute about 5 minutes, breaking the meat up with a wooden spoon, until lightly browned.
  6. Turn off the heat. Tip the meat into a bowl and scrape any brown bits out of the pot.
  7. Back on low heat, add the oil and the soffritto. Cook 5 to 8 minutes, stirring often, until soft but with no color. Work in the tomato paste for 1 to 2 minutes, then return the meat and stir it through.
  8. Pour in the wine and cook 2 to 3 minutes, until it has dropped by about half and the alcohol has gone.
  9. Add the water and hold the pot at a very low simmer for 2 to 3 hours. Look at it every 30 minutes and top up with water so it never dries out, browns or catches on the base. It is done when the meat is completely soft, the flavors have come together, and the ragù coats the spoon thickly.
  10. Off the heat, stir the milk in. The ragù should take on a sheen.
  11. Weigh out 512 g of ragù for the pasta. The rest keeps 5 days in a sealed container in the refrigerator, or freezes.
  12. Make 624 g of tagliatelle from the egg dough.
  13. Salt a large pot of water heavily and bring it to a boil over high heat.
  14. Set a large saute pan over low heat and stir the 512 g of ragù together with 1 to 2 ladles of pasta water (55 to 115 g).
  15. Once it is warm, stir in the butter and set the pan aside.
  16. Cook the tagliatelle 30 seconds to 1 minute. It should come out tender, not soft.
  17. Lift the pasta into the saute pan with tongs or a pasta basket and raise the heat to medium. Add another 1 to 2 ladles of pasta water (55 to 115 g) and fold gently with a large spoon for 30 seconds to 1 minute. A splash more water loosens it if it tightens.
  18. Off the heat, fold in half the parmigiano, loosening with a little more pasta water if it needs it. The meat ought to cling to the pasta, with the sauce still glossy.
  19. Divide between bowls and finish with the rest of the parmigiano.

Notes

  • Tomato paste rather than tinned tomato is the choice here, because it brings sweetness and tomato flavor without putting liquid into the pot.
  • Wringing the ground soffritto in cheesecloth drives off its water, so the vegetables soften rather than stew.
  • The meat is browned, lifted out, and the pot scraped clean before the soffritto goes in, so the ragù never picks up a roasted edge over the following hours.
  • The batch makes far more ragù than the dish needs. Only 512 g is dressed with pasta.
  • Leftover dressed pasta fries well. Oil in a pan over high heat, both sides, until it crusts, which is tagliatelle ripassate in padella.
