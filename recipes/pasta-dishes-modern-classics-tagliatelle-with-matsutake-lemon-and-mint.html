Tagliatelle with Matsutake Mushrooms, Lemon, and Mint

Shape
Tagliatelle
Serves
4-6
Total Time
1 h 15 min plus the dough rest
Source
Missy Robbins, Pasta

Print card (PDF)

Ingredients

Batch
  • Egg dough: 1 batch
  • Matsutake mushrooms: 454 g
  • Olive oil, for the mushrooms: 168 g
  • Olive oil, for the garlic: 14 g
  • Garlic, finely chopped: 25 g
  • Unsalted butter, cold and cubed: 60 g
  • Parmigiano-reggiano, finely grated: 45 g
  • Lemon, peel finely chopped and pith removed: 1
  • Parsley, finely chopped: 20 g
  • Mint leaves: 30
  • Black pepper: quantity not given

Method

  1. Cut 624 g of tagliatelle from the egg dough.
  2. Clean the mushrooms by paring the first layer of flesh off the stems with a paring knife, taking as little as you can. Wipe the rest of the dirt away with a slightly damp towel, gently but thoroughly.
  3. Slice the mushrooms very thinly, cutting through caps and stems alike, to pieces around 1/8 inch thick and 2 inches long.
  4. Set a large saute pan over high heat with 56 g of the olive oil. Lay a third of the mushrooms into the pan in an even layer and sear them 5 to 7 minutes, turning them so the color goes on evenly, until the edges are golden brown and they are cooked through. They will still feel firm because matsutakes are so meaty, but they take on a gloss. Move them to a plate or bowl.
  5. Repeat twice more, 56 g of oil to each batch, until all the mushrooms are done. Keep the pan.
  6. Salt a large pot of water heavily and bring it to the boil.
  7. While it heats, put the pan back over low heat with the last 14 g of oil. Give the garlic 10 to 15 seconds, until aromatic but without color, then return all the mushrooms and stir them through.
  8. Cook the tagliatelle 30 seconds to 1 minute. It wants to be tender, not soft.
  9. While it cooks, add 2 to 3 ladles of pasta water, 115 g to 170 g, and the butter to the mushroom pan, and swirl to emulsify.
  10. Lift the pasta over with tongs or a pasta basket, raise the heat to medium and toss 1 to 2 minutes to marry.
  11. Off the heat, add half the parmigiano, then the lemon peel and parsley, tossing as you go. A splash of pasta water loosens the sauce if it tightens. It is ready when it clings with a gloss.
  12. Divide into bowls and finish with the remaining parmigiano, the mint leaves and pepper.

Notes

  • Matsutake season is short, September through November, and they are hard to find even inside it. Porcini or king oyster, also sold as trumpet royale, both substitute. Their flavor differs but both are meaty enough that the texture of the dish survives.
  • Sear the mushrooms in three batches with fresh oil each time. A crowded pan steams them instead of coloring them.
  • Matsutakes stay firm when they are cooked through. Judge them by the gloss, not by softness.
  • Mint stands in for nepitella, a perennial herb whose aroma sits between mint and marjoram.
