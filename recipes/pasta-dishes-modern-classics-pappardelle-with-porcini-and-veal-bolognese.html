Pasta / Dishes

Pappardelle with Porcini and Veal Bolognese

Shape
Pappardelle
Serves
4-6
Total Time
4 h plus the dough rest
Source
Missy Robbins, Pasta

Print card (PDF)

Ingredients

Batch
  • Egg dough: 1 batch
  • Dried porcini mushrooms: 56 g
  • Room-temperature water: 1.4 kg
  • Fennel bulb: 200 g
  • Onion: 236 g
  • Garlic: 10 g
  • Olive oil, for the veal: 28 g
  • Olive oil, for the soffritto: 112 g
  • Ground veal: 896 g
  • Dry white wine: 220 g
  • Juice from canned San Marzano tomatoes: 122 g
  • Fresh bay leaf: 1
  • Thyme: 2 sprigs
  • Rosemary: 2 sprigs
  • Sage: 1 sprig
  • Coriander seed: 2 g
  • Black peppercorns: 9 g
  • Unsalted butter, cold and cubed: 30 g
  • Parsley, finely chopped: 20 g
  • Parmigiano-reggiano, finely grated: 22 g
  • Whole nutmeg: quantity not given
  • Salt: quantity not given
  • Black pepper: quantity not given

Method

  1. For the ragù, cover the porcini with cool water in a bowl and soak them 5 minutes, sloshing them around with your hand a few times to shift the dirt. Drain, return them to the bowl, pour the 1.4 kg of room-temperature water over and leave them 30 minutes to 1 hour to soften.
  2. Once they are fully hydrated, drain them and keep the soaking liquid, which should come to about 948 g. Chop the mushrooms finely. Keep mushrooms and liquid separate.
  3. While the porcini soak, fit the small die to a meat grinder. Core the fennel and strip off its fronds and stems. Cut the fennel and onion into ½- to 1-inch pieces. Pass the garlic, onion and fennel through the grinder, then gather the ground mixture in cheesecloth and wring the liquid out of it. That is the soffritto.
  4. Warm the 28 g of oil over medium-high heat in a heavy Dutch oven or sauce pot. Add the veal and cook 5 to 7 minutes, crumbling it into small even pieces with a wooden spoon, to just cooked through. Lift it out with a slotted spoon into a bowl.
  5. Turn the heat to low. Add the remaining 112 g of oil, the soffritto and the chopped porcini. Cook 3 to 5 minutes, stirring often, until softened but still pale.
  6. Pour in the wine and cook about 5 minutes, until it has nearly all gone.
  7. Put the veal back in, then pour in the porcini liquid you reserved along with the San Marzano juice.
  8. Tie the peppercorns, coriander seed, bay leaf, sage, rosemary and thyme into a cheesecloth sachet and drop it in.
  9. Cook on low 2 to 3 hours. The flavors should have melded and the meat should be completely soft.
  10. Off the heat, salt to taste. Measure 606 g of ragù out for this dish. The rest keeps in an airtight container, 5 days refrigerated or a month frozen.
  11. Cut 624 g of pappardelle from the egg dough.
  12. Salt a large pot of water heavily and bring it to the boil over high heat.
  13. Set a large saute pan over medium-low heat and add the measured ragù.
  14. Cook the pappardelle 1 to 2 minutes. The ribbons want to be tender, not soft.
  15. Lift the pasta over with tongs or a pasta basket, raise the heat to medium and add the butter and one ladle of pasta water, 55 g. Work pasta and sauce together for about a minute, alternating between tossing and folding with a wooden spoon. A splash more pasta water loosens it if it tightens. Fold the parsley through. The ragù should coat the ribbons without clumping on them.
  16. Divide into bowls and finish with the parmigiano, grated nutmeg and pepper to taste.

Notes

  • The porcini soaking water is an ingredient. About 948 g of it goes into the ragù and carries most of the mushroom flavor.
  • The mushrooms get soaked twice. The first 5 minutes in cool water is a wash for grit, the long soak is what softens them.
  • Veal is milder and softer than beef or pork, which is the point. There is no milk and no tomato paste either, just a splash of tomato juice, so the porcini and the veal are not buried under fat or sweetness.
  • Wringing the ground soffritto dry in cheesecloth stops the ragù stewing in vegetable water.
  • 606 g of ragù dresses this pasta. The batch makes considerably more than that.
