Agnolotti dal Plin with Brisket and Caramelized Onion

Filling
Brisket and Caramelized Onion Filling
Shape
Agnolotti dal Plin
Region
North
Serves
6-8
Total Time
8 h plus the dough rest
Source
Missy Robbins, Pasta

Print card (PDF)

Ingredients

Batch
  • Salt: 27 g
  • Brisket, center cut with a moderate amount of fat: 912 g
  • Olive oil: 56 g
  • Onions, cut into 1/8-inch slices: 1.85 kg
  • Thyme: 2 sprigs
  • Water: 474 g
  • Parmigiano-reggiano for the filling, finely grated: 30 g
  • Nutmeg, whole: quantity not given
  • Egg dough: 1 batch
  • Unsalted butter, cold and cubed: 85 g
  • Parmigiano-reggiano to finish, finely grated: 22 g

Method

  1. Salt the brisket on every side. Give it an hour at minimum to take the salt up, or refrigerate it as long as 24 hours. Bring it back to room temperature before cooking.
  2. Heat the oven to 275°F.
  3. Pat the brisket dry. Warm 42 g of the oil in a large saute pan over medium heat and sear the brisket fat side down 4 to 5 minutes, until the fat begins to melt and goes golden brown. Sear the other sides 4 to 5 minutes each, turning as needed, then transfer it to a sheet pan.
  4. Spread half the onions over the base of a heavy lidded saucepot or Dutch oven. Set the brisket on them, pile the rest of the onions over it, and lay the thyme on top. Pour in the water, seal the pot with foil or parchment, and clamp the lid down over the seal.
  5. Cook 5 to 6 hours, until the meat pulls apart easily under a fork. By then the onions will have collapsed and caramelized into a jus.
  6. While that cooks, chill the meat grinder and its small die in the refrigerator at least 1 hour, or 30 minutes in the freezer.
  7. Take the pot out, uncover it, and spoon off whatever fat has risen to the surface. Leave the brisket in the pot about 20 minutes, until cool enough to handle.
  8. Lift the brisket onto a sheet pan. Strain the cooking liquid, keeping solids and liquid separate and squeezing the onions hard. You should have 150 g of onions and 474 g of liquid. Set both aside.
  9. Pick the meat into small pieces and discard the fat. Grind the meat through the small die, then grind the braised onions.
  10. Combine the meat and onions in a bowl with the 30 g of parmigiano and the remaining 14 g of oil. Season with grated nutmeg and salt and mix well. Aim for properly seasoned. Cover it and refrigerate.
  11. Take the filling out about 30 minutes before you use it, because it is far easier to work at room temperature.
  12. Make 160 to 180 agnolotti dal plin from the egg dough and the filling.
  13. Salt a large pot of water heavily and bring it to a boil over high heat.
  14. Set a large saute pan on low heat. Put in the strained braising liquid with the butter and swirl them into a sauce. Hold it on low, swirling now and then.
  15. Drop the agnolotti in and bring the water down to a gentle simmer rather than a rolling boil, which matters here. Cook 2 to 4 minutes. They are done when the thickest seal has gone tender.
  16. Lift them into the saute pan with a spider or pasta basket and raise the heat to medium. Swirl 1 to 2 minutes, turning the pieces with a spoon, until the sauce has thickened to something close to gravy and hugs the pasta. A splash of pasta water loosens it if it tightens.
  17. Divide into flat-bottomed bowls. Spoon the sauce left in the pan over the top and finish with the 22 g of parmigiano and grated nutmeg.

Notes

  • The braise is both filling and sauce. Strained solids go inside the pasta, the 474 g of strained liquid becomes the sugo the agnolotti are finished in.
  • 275°F under a sealed lid for 5 to 6 hours takes the meat through roasting, then steaming, then braising as the onions melt down.
  • Squeeze the onions hard when straining. Liquid left in them comes straight off the sauce.
  • Simmer, do not boil. Judge doneness at the thickest closure point.
  • 160 to 180 pieces serves 6 to 8, which is 25 to 30 postage-stamp pockets a head. This is not a same-day dish.
