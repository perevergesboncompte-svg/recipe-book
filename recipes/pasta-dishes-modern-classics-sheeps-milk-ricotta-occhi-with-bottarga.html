Sheep's Milk Ricotta-Filled Occhi with Lemon and Bottarga

Filling
Sheep's and Cow's Milk Ricotta Filling
Shape
Occhi
Serves
6-8
Total Time
1 h plus the dough rest
Source
Missy Robbins, Pasta

Print card (PDF)

Ingredients

Batch
  • Sheep's milk ricotta, whipped: 348 g
  • Cow's milk ricotta: 348 g
  • Salt: quantity not given
  • Egg dough: 1 batch
  • Unsalted butter, cold and cubed: 75 g
  • Lemon, peel finely chopped and pith removed: 1
  • Bottarga: 1/4 lobe

Method

  1. Put both ricottas in a food processor and pulse only until the mixture turns as smooth as cake frosting and picks up a sheen. Stop there. Keep going and it breaks.
  2. Scrape it into a bowl and salt it until it tastes properly seasoned. Refrigerate until you shape the pasta.
  3. Make 90 occhi from the egg dough and the ricotta filling.
  4. Salt a large pot of water heavily and bring it to the boil.
  5. Add the occhi, then pull the heat back so the water holds a gentle simmer instead of a rolling boil. Shape and filling are both fragile and high heat will tear them. Cook 1 to 2 minutes, until the thickest closure point is tender and the filling runs when you cut one open.
  6. While they cook, set a large saute pan over very low heat with the butter and 2 to 3 ladles of pasta water, 115 g to 170 g. Swirl to emulsify.
  7. Carry the pasta over with a spider and swirl 30 to 45 seconds to marry, turning the pieces gently with a spoon so every side gets coated. A splash of pasta water loosens the sauce if it tightens. It is ready when it clings with a gloss.
  8. Lay the occhi out in a single layer on a platter or on plates. Slacken what is left in the pan with a few drops of pasta water and spoon it over. Finish with the chopped lemon peel and a generous grating of bottarga from a Microplane.

Notes

  • Look for grey mullet bottarga. It runs milder and less fishy than the other kinds.
  • Over-processing breaks the ricotta. Pulse only to the point where it looks like cake frosting and shines.
  • The sheep's milk ricotta goes in whipped, the cow's milk ricotta as it comes.
  • Fish alongside cheese is no transgression in the Italian south, where ricotta and cured roe are both staples.
  • Occhi are thin at the closure. Keep the water at a simmer or they split and lose the filling.
