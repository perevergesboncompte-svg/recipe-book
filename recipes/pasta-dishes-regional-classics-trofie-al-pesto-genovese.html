Pasta / Dishes

Trofie al Pesto Genovese

Shape
Trofie
Region
North
Serves
4-6
Total Time
1 h 30 min plus the dough rest
Source
Missy Robbins, Pasta

Print card (PDF)

Ingredients

Batch
  • Semolina dough: 1 batch
  • Pine nuts: 180 g
  • Basil leaves, plus more to garnish: 210 g
  • Garlic: 5 g
  • Olive oil: 150 g
  • Parmigiano-reggiano, finely grated: 36 g
  • Fiore sardo, finely grated, plus more to finish: 36 g
  • Pecorino romano, finely grated: 36 g

Method

  1. Roll 624 g of trofie from the semolina dough.
  2. Set the oven to 325°F. Scatter the pine nuts across a sheet pan in one layer and toast them 5 to 7 minutes, to a very pale gold. Look at them every couple of minutes so they do not catch. Let them cool, about 15 minutes.
  3. Put a large pot of water on to boil over high heat and salt it heavily. Sit a colander in an ice water bath alongside.
  4. Drop the basil into the pot for 5 seconds, lift it straight out and plunge it into the ice water to stop it cooking.
  5. Squeeze the water out of the basil and puree it smooth in a high-speed blender. A splash of water gets the blades moving if it stalls.
  6. Add 150 g of the pine nuts and blend until they disappear into the puree.
  7. Scrape the basil and pine nut puree into a large mixing bowl. Grate the garlic clove in on a Microplane. Fold in the olive oil and all three cheeses until the pesto is smooth and even.
  8. Weigh out 320 g of pesto and leave it in the bowl for the pasta. The remainder keeps 3 days refrigerated in a container under a layer of olive oil. Coarsely chop the last 30 g of pine nuts.
  9. Bring a second large pot of water to the boil over high heat and salt it heavily.
  10. Cook the trofie 3 to 5 minutes, until tender with some chew left in them.
  11. Lift the pasta straight into the bowl of pesto with a spider and turn it gently. Add 55 to 78 g of the pasta water a little at a time, until the pesto coats every piece easily.
  12. Divide into bowls and finish with more grated fiore sardo, the chopped pine nuts and basil leaves.

Notes

  • Pesto al Genovese runs on seven things: salt, garlic, olive oil, Genovese basil, pine nuts, parmigiano-reggiano and pecorino sardo. Fiore sardo, the gently smoky Sardinian pecorino, is what carries this version.
  • Five seconds in boiling water and a plunge into ice sets the color of the basil, so the pesto stays green instead of going dark.
  • Trofie is the shape pesto meets most often along the Ligurian Riviera. Linguine, called trenette there, is the other common pairing.
  • Versions along that coast vary widely. Some add green beans and potatoes to the pot, and the pesto itself ranges from chunky and raw out of a mortar to as smooth as peanut butter.
